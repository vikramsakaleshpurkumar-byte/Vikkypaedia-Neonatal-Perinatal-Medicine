<details><summary>Why does neonatal anesthesia require developmental expertise?</summary>

Immature organ systems and altered drug handling. Anatomy, physiology, homeostasis and pharmacology differ between adults, term neonates and preterm neonates.

</details>

<details><summary>Which neonatal airway proportions matter during airway planning?</summary>

Relatively large head and tongue with a short neck and higher anterior larynx. Neonatal proportions affect positioning and airway management; these differences extend beyond size.

PDF p. 682
</details>

<details><summary>Why does a fall in heart rate threaten neonatal cardiac output?</summary>

Limited ability to augment stroke volume. The neonatal heart has limited stroke-volume reserve, making cardiac output strongly dependent on heart rate.

PDF p. 683
</details>

<details><summary>How does anesthesia increase neonatal heat-loss risk?</summary>

Blunted thermoregulation plus cutaneous vasodilation. Anesthesia interferes with thermal defenses and increases heat loss; open cavities add risk.

PDF p. 684
</details>

<details><summary>Why can hypoglycemia be missed during neonatal anesthesia?</summary>

Limited reserves and masked clinical signs under anesthesia. Neonates have high glucose demand and limited reserves, while anesthesia masks usual signs.

PDF p. 684
</details>

<details><summary>Why may a very small sick preterm infant need prolonged postoperative respiratory support?</summary>

Immature clearance prolongs anesthetic effects, illness and prematurity complicate emergence, and hypothermia can prolong neuromuscular blockade; ongoing NICU care may be needed.

</details>
