<details><summary>How does the Donabedian framework organize quality measures?</summary>

Structure, process and outcome. Structure concerns the care setting, process concerns care activities, and outcomes are their results.

</details>

<details><summary>What are the core elements of the Model for Improvement?</summary>

Measurable aim, tracked measures, changes and PDSA cycles. The model combines specific aims, measures over time, strategic change ideas and iterative PDSA learning.

PDF p. 90
</details>

<details><summary>Why are balancing measures needed?</summary>

Track growth as a balancing measure alongside the target. Balancing measures detect related outcomes that may worsen while the target improves; the chapter gives this feeding-growth example.

PDF p. 94
</details>

<details><summary>How should shared care baselines handle individual patient needs?</summary>

Adapt care, document the deviation and learn from it. Shared baselines reduce unwarranted variation while allowing justified individualization and subsequent refinement.

PDF p. 91
</details>

<details><summary>Why do stable agreed definitions matter in neonatal quality measurement?</summary>

Reconcile definitions and assess comparability. Clear, stable definitions across time and location are essential for interpreting differences and comparing NICUs.

PDF p. 92
</details>

<details><summary>What should a NICU quality review include besides quantitative outcome data?</summary>

Input from staff and families and examination of the local context and care processes; these inform where and how improvement is attempted.
