<details><summary>Why is albumin binding important in unconjugated bilirubin physiology?</summary>

Albumin binding influences the unbound fraction available to enter tissues. Unconjugated bilirubin is poorly water soluble and circulates bound to albumin; binding capacity influences biologic availability.

</details>

<details><summary>Which signs are concerning for acute bilirubin encephalopathy?</summary>

Acute bilirubin encephalopathy. This constellation is described in acute bilirubin encephalopathy and calls for urgent management of severe hyperbilirubinemia.

PDF p. 1896
</details>

<details><summary>What chemical changes underlie bilirubin phototherapy?</summary>

Photoisomerization including formation of more readily eliminated bilirubin isomers. Light changes bilirubin configuration and structure, including formation of lumirubin, facilitating elimination.

PDF p. 1908
</details>

<details><summary>Which patient variables are necessary for the chapter’s 2022 phototherapy threshold graphs?</summary>

Postnatal age in hours, gestational age and relevant neurotoxicity risk factors. The reproduced graphs separate gestation, age in hours and risk-factor groups; an isolated bilirubin number is insufficient.

PDF p. 1923
</details>

<details><summary>Is conjugated hyperbilirubinemia physiologic neonatal jaundice?</summary>

A pathologic bile secretion or flow problem requiring evaluation. The chapter calls conjugated hyperbilirubinemia always pathologic; its differential includes obstructive, hepatocellular, infectious and metabolic disease.

PDF p. 1922
</details>

<details><summary>Why can normal stool pigmentation very early in jaundice fail to exclude biliary atresia?</summary>

Acholic stools may appear only later; clinical obstruction and even biopsy features can be nonspecific early, so evolving cholestasis needs timely evaluation.

PDF p. 1928
</details>
