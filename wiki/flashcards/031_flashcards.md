<details><summary>Why is establishing functional residual capacity central to newborn transition?</summary>

Replacement of lung liquid by air and establishment of FRC. Lung aeration and FRC support gas exchange while pulmonary blood flow increases.

PDF p. 558
</details>

<details><summary>Which positive-pressure device can function without an external gas supply?</summary>

Self-inflating bag. Self-inflating bags re-expand after compression and can operate without a gas supply.

PDF p. 561
</details>

<details><summary>How does a T-piece device switch between peak inflation and PEEP/CPAP?</summary>

Occlude the T-piece opening. Occluding the opening delivers set peak pressure; leaving it open permits PEEP or CPAP.

PDF p. 562
</details>

<details><summary>What facial area should a newborn resuscitation mask cover?</summary>

Mouth and nose without covering eyes or extending beyond the chin. Correct size and positioning over mouth and nose help limit leaks; eyes and chin should not be overlapped.

PDF p. 563
</details>

<details><summary>Why does PEEP help maintain preterm lung volume?</summary>

Maintain end-expiratory volume and reduce distal airway collapse. PEEP helps maintain FRC and reduces end-expiratory collapse; physiologic rationale does not prove every clinical outcome.

PDF p. 568
</details>

<details><summary>What can a respiratory function monitor add during mask ventilation?</summary>

It can help assess mask leak and delivered tidal volume, which clinicians may estimate inaccurately by observation alone.

PDF p. 564
</details>
