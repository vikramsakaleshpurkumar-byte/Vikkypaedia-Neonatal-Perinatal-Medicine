<details><summary>Which cultures matter in evaluating invasive neonatal candidiasis?</summary>

Blood, urine and CSF cultures as clinically appropriate. Sterile-site cultures remain central; urine and CSF may reveal infection not shown by blood culture.

<details><summary>Can normal CSF counts exclude neonatal fungal CNS infection?</summary>

CSF culture still matters because routine indices may be normal. CNS candidiasis can occur without candidemia and without abnormal routine CSF chemistries or counts.

<details><summary>Why does catheter removal matter during Candida bloodstream infection?</summary>

Prompt removal of the implicated central catheter when feasible. Prompt removal is associated with improved clearance and outcomes; Candida biofilms can resist drug penetration.

PDF p. 900
</details>

<details><summary>What should persistent candidemia prompt clinicians to investigate?</summary>

Look for infected thrombi and end-organ abscesses. Persistent candidemia increases concern for dissemination, including catheter-tip thrombi and abdominal or brain foci.

PDF p. 893
</details>

<details><summary>How do gestational timing and severity relate in congenital toxoplasmosis?</summary>

Transmission rises later, while earlier fetal infection tends to be more severe. The probability of transmission and severity move in different directions with gestation.

PDF p. 917
</details>

<details><summary>What exposure routes transmit Toxoplasma to humans?</summary>

Ingesting tissue cysts in raw or poorly cooked meat, or oocysts from contaminated food or water, including contamination associated with cat feces.
