<details><summary>Which maternal immunoglobulin class crosses the placenta?</summary>

IgG. Maternal IgG crosses the placenta, unlike IgM and IgA in the source’s description.

PDF p. 367
</details>

<details><summary>How does fetal-neonatal alloimmune thrombocytopenia develop?</summary>

Maternal IgG against a fetal platelet alloantigen. Maternal sensitization to an antigen-positive fetus produces IgG that crosses back and destroys fetal platelets.

PDF p. 369
</details>

<details><summary>Can FNAIT occur in a first pregnancy?</summary>

A first pregnancy does not exclude FNAIT. The source reports that many FNAIT cases occur during the first pregnancy of at-risk couples.

PDF p. 369
</details>

<details><summary>Should treatment of clinically suspected serious FNAIT wait for complete immunologic testing?</summary>

Start indicated management on clinical suspicion without waiting for the workup. The source states that suspected cases should be treated based on clinical diagnosis, with management shaped by count, bleeding and risk.

PDF p. 369
</details>

<details><summary>Which maternal antibodies are emphasized in neonatal lupus?</summary>

Anti-Ro and anti-La. The source focuses on anti-Ro and anti-La in neonatal lupus.

PDF p. 371
</details>

<details><summary>Why may the mother be clinically disease-free in an alloimmune fetal disorder?</summary>

The maternal immune response targets an exogenous fetal antigen rather than necessarily injuring maternal tissue.
