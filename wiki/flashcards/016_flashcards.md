<details><summary>Why is gestational hypertension a provisional diagnosis?</summary>

It may resolve, progress to preeclampsia or reveal chronic hypertension. The group includes transient disease, evolving preeclampsia and previously unrecognized chronic hypertension.

PDF p. 305
</details>

<details><summary>What laboratory triad defines HELLP syndrome?</summary>

HELLP syndrome. HELLP denotes hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelets; hypertension or proteinuria need not both be present.

PDF p. 307
</details>

<details><summary>Why is surveillance needed even without initial severe preeclampsia features?</summary>

Progression may be unpredictable and rapid. Progression can occur within hours to days, and postpartum presentation is also possible.

PDF p. 311
</details>

<details><summary>Which factors shape the decision to deliver in preeclampsia?</summary>

Gestational age, disease severity and maternal-fetal well-being. Delivery treats the maternal disease, but prematurity creates fetal risks; timing requires integrated assessment.

PDF p. 312
</details>

<details><summary>Why is magnesium sulfate used in severe preeclampsia?</summary>

Prevention of eclamptic seizures. Magnesium sulfate is supported for seizure prophylaxis in severe preeclampsia.

PDF p. 313
</details>

<details><summary>What longer-term follow-up issue follows a history of preeclampsia?</summary>

An increased later cardiovascular risk supports ongoing surveillance and prevention; the association does not resolve whether shared risks or preeclampsia itself drive all of the excess risk.

</details>
