<details><summary>What do otoacoustic emissions primarily assess?</summary>

Outer hair cells. OAE reflects outer hair-cell function; outer or middle-ear obstruction can interfere with measurement.

PDF p. 1159
</details>

<details><summary>Why can an infant pass OAE but fail ABR?</summary>

Neural hearing loss with preserved outer hair-cell function. OAE can miss neural disease involving inner hair cells, auditory nerve or brainstem pathways.

PDF p. 1159
</details>

<details><summary>Does failing the newborn hearing screen prove permanent hearing loss?</summary>

Arrange timely follow-up because transient conductive interference can cause failure. A screening result requires follow-up; transient outer or middle-ear dysfunction can cause false-positive failure.

PDF p. 1159
</details>

<details><summary>When should early intervention referral occur after confirmed hearing loss?</summary>

Refer to early intervention at diagnosis, including unilateral loss. Early individualized family-centered services are recommended for unilateral and bilateral hearing loss.

PDF p. 1161
</details>

<details><summary>Why continue hearing and language surveillance after a passed newborn screen?</summary>

Audiologic and speech-language assessment because delayed or progressive loss can occur. Mild loss may be missed and some causes, including CMV, can produce delayed or progressive hearing loss.

PDF p. 1165
</details>

<details><summary>What role does genetics evaluation have after confirmed infant hearing loss?</summary>

Families should be offered evaluation and counseling to investigate cause, associated disorders, prognosis and recurrence considerations.

PDF p. 1163
</details>
