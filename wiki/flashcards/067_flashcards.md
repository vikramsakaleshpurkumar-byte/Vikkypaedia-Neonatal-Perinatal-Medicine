<details><summary>Why can respiratory failure persist beyond lung compression in diaphragmatic hernia?</summary>

Pulmonary hypoplasia with abnormal vascular development. Both lungs can have reduced airspace and vascular development, particularly on the involved side, with increased vascular resistance.

<details><summary>Must neonatal chylous fluid be milky before feeds begin?</summary>

Clear fluid does not exclude chylothorax before enteral feeding. Chylous fluid becomes characteristically milky with established feeding; fluid analysis helps diagnosis.

PDF p. 1279
</details>

<details><summary>What blood-supply finding helps identify pulmonary sequestration?</summary>

Bronchopulmonary sequestration. Systemic arterial supply is a distinguishing feature of sequestrated nonfunctioning lung tissue.

PDF p. 1282
</details>

<details><summary>How can meconium aspiration impair surfactant function?</summary>

Inflammatory injury and alveolar protein leak can damage type II cells and inactivate surfactant. Aspiration injury combines obstruction, inflammation and impaired surfactant production or function.

PDF p. 1285
</details>

<details><summary>What mechanism and time course characterize typical TTN?</summary>

Delayed clearance of fetal lung liquid. Typical TTN reflects delayed liquid absorption and usually resolves over the first days; early pneumonia and other causes can overlap.

PDF p. 1289
</details>

<details><summary>Why should the early label TTN remain provisional in a symptomatic newborn?</summary>

Its early presentation can overlap with pneumonia and other respiratory disease; clinical course and appropriate evaluation are needed.
