<details><summary>What is the treatment principle for sinus tachycardia?</summary>

Identify and treat the underlying cause. Sinus tachycardia is usually a response to conditions such as fever, infection or anemia; management targets the cause.

<details><summary>What baseline ECG features indicate a manifest accessory pathway?</summary>

Antegrade conduction over a manifest accessory pathway. Early ventricular activation through a manifest pathway produces pre-excitation; a concealed retrograde-only pathway has no baseline delta wave.

PDF p. 1456
</details>

<details><summary>Why can ventricular rate be much slower than atrial rate during neonatal flutter?</summary>

Decremental atrioventricular nodal conduction. The AV node limits conduction of the rapid atrial rhythm, often in 2:1, 3:1 or 4:1 patterns.

PDF p. 1455
</details>

<details><summary>What ECG sequence characterizes Mobitz type I block?</summary>

Mobitz type I second-degree block. Progressive PR lengthening before a dropped beat is the Wenckebach pattern.

PDF p. 1465
</details>

<details><summary>What must be checked before attributing a prolonged QT interval solely to congenital long QT syndrome?</summary>

Review QT-prolonging medicines and electrolyte disturbances. Medicines and hypocalcemia or hypokalemia may cause or exacerbate QT prolongation; both acquired and congenital forms can cause dangerous arrhythmias.

PDF p. 1467
</details>

<details><summary>Why should very fast irregular atrial tachycardia prompt review of a central-line tip?</summary>

A catheter tip in the atrium can mechanically stimulate atrial tachycardia; line position should be assessed before assuming an intrinsic arrhythmia substrate.
