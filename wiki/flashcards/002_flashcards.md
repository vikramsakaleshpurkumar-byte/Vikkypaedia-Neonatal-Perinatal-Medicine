<details><summary>Why should relative risk be accompanied by absolute risk?</summary>

Relative effects match, but absolute differences differ. The absolute increases are 0.1 and 10 percentage points. Equal relative effects need not have equal clinical impact.

<details><summary>Why is single-factor causality difficult to infer for common prematurity morbidities?</summary>

Multiple component causes and their sequence may contribute. The text describes IVH, NEC and BPD as multifactorial, with causes that can be unknown, unmeasured or time dependent.

<details><summary>What is the purpose of epidemiology in neonatal-perinatal medicine?</summary>

Epidemiology. Epidemiology studies population health and disease, risk factors and associations, including mortality as an indicator of health and care.

<details><summary>Which signs are included in the cited definition of live birth?</summary>

A definite sign of life such as heartbeat. Breathing or another definite sign of life after delivery qualifies; cord cutting and placental separation are not prerequisites.

</details>

<details><summary>What can maternal and infant mortality indicators help assess?</summary>

Assess population health, access and quality in context. Mortality rates inform assessment of population health and care systems, but interpretation requires context and consistent definitions.
