<details><summary>How can disrupted lung development contribute to later pulmonary hypertension?</summary>

Reduced alveolar-capillary growth with vascular dysfunction and remodeling. Reduced vessel number and abnormal growth interact with hypoxia, hypercarbia and other stressors to elevate vascular resistance.

PDF p. 1349
</details>

<details><summary>Does absence of a measurable tricuspid-regurgitation jet exclude pulmonary hypertension?</summary>

Pulmonary hypertension is not excluded; assess other findings and the clinical picture. The chapter notes that absent measurable TRJV does not rule out pulmonary hypertension; other signs and longitudinal evaluation matter.

PDF p. 1349
</details>

<details><summary>How can wheeze after preterm birth differ from typical allergic asthma?</summary>

Prematurity-related airway injury and altered development can contribute. Preterm wheezing phenotypes can differ from term allergic asthma and occur with or without BPD.

PDF p. 1351
</details>

<details><summary>Why include preterm birth in an adult respiratory history?</summary>

Reduced lung growth and altered lifelong function after prematurity. Developmental lung impairment and subsequent exposures can influence the trajectory toward adult obstructive disease.

PDF p. 1353
</details>

<details><summary>Why measure respiratory outcomes beyond NICU BPD status in trials?</summary>

Longer-term respiratory benefit still needs direct assessment. BPD or discharge support is not a sufficient surrogate for all postneonatal airway outcomes.

PDF p. 1352
</details>

<details><summary>Why does the source describe optimal oxygen targets in postneonatal pulmonary hypertension as an evidence gap?</summary>

Guideline targets differ and have not been systematically studied in this specific population; source recommendations should be read with that limitation.

PDF p. 1349
</details>
