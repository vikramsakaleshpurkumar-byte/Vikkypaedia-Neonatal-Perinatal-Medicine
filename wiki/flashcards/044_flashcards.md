<details><summary>What is the central purpose of perinatal palliative care?</summary>

Optimize comfort and quality of life while supporting family decisions. Palliative care coordinates multidisciplinary support for infant comfort and caregiver needs throughout the decision process.

PDF p. 761
</details>

<details><summary>Which NICU groups can benefit from palliative care?</summary>

Palliative assessment of benefits, burdens and family goals. The chapter includes long-suffering NICU patients, extreme prematurity and life-limiting diagnoses among potential beneficiaries.

PDF p. 762
</details>

<details><summary>Are perinatal resuscitation preferences necessarily all-or-none and permanent?</summary>

Define a treatment trial and revisit goals as response becomes known. The source describes a spectrum of interventions and opportunities to reassess clinical response and goals.

PDF p. 765
</details>

<details><summary>Which nonpharmacologic measures can support neonatal comfort?</summary>

Holding, swaddling and non-nutritive sucking. Simple noninvasive measures may reduce distress and continue as adjuncts when medication is required.

PDF p. 767
</details>

<details><summary>What is the therapeutic goal when treating end-of-life dyspnea?</summary>

Relieve dyspnea using the comfort plan, including appropriate opioids. For end-of-life dyspnea the goal is comfort; opioids are described as a mainstay of symptom relief.

PDF p. 769
</details>

<details><summary>What should clinicians discuss when a desired resuscitative intervention may be technically impossible?</summary>

Discuss feasibility and alternatives antenatally when anticipated, and revisit goals as the infant’s condition and response become clearer.

PDF p. 765
</details>
