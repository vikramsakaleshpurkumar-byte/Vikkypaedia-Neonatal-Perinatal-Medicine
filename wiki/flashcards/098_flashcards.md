<details><summary>Which physiologic roles make skin integrity especially important in newborns?</summary>

Barrier to water loss and infection control. Skin supports water balance, thermoregulation and host defense; injury can therefore have systemic consequences.

Erythema toxicum. The source describes an evanescent, sterile, eosinophil-rich benign eruption, usually arising after birth.

<details><summary>What sequence characterizes transient neonatal pustular melanosis?</summary>

Transient neonatal pustular melanosis. The eruption progresses from superficial pustules to collarettes and residual pigmented macules, and stages may coexist at birth.

<details><summary>What is emphasized for postnatal diagnosis of epidermolysis bullosa?</summary>

Next-generation sequencing, with selected biopsy-based studies for special situations. The source describes genetic diagnosis, with immunofluorescence mapping or electron microscopy reserved for selected situations.

<details><summary>What mechanism links neonatal lupus skin disease and congenital heart block?</summary>

Transplacental maternal autoantibodies. Maternal anti-Ro/SSA or anti-La/SSB antibodies can produce transient cutaneous disease and congenital heart block; mothers may be asymptomatic.

</details>

<details><summary>How does palm and sole involvement differ between two common benign pustular eruptions?</summary>

Erythema toxicum rarely affects palms or soles, whereas transient neonatal pustular melanosis can occur there; morphology, timing and the infant’s condition still guide the assessment.
