<details><summary>Should gastric residuals alone stop feeds in an otherwise well infant?</summary>

Residual volume alone does not justify stopping feeds. The chapter finds no evidence supporting discontinuation solely for residuals in the absence of other symptoms.

<details><summary>What milk alternative is recommended when mother’s own milk is unavailable?</summary>

Pasteurized donor human milk with attention to nutrient adequacy. Pasteurized donor milk is recommended; fortification and growth monitoring address compositional differences.

<details><summary>Why is human milk fortified for many premature infants?</summary>

Milk alone may not supply enough protein and minerals for preterm needs. Preterm requirements can exceed milk supplies of protein, calcium, phosphorus and other nutrients; fortification improves growth.

</details>

<details><summary>How does milk protein content change during lactation, and why does that matter?</summary>

Protein content declines over the first weeks. Fixed assumptions about baseline milk composition can overestimate delivered protein, particularly with donor milk.
