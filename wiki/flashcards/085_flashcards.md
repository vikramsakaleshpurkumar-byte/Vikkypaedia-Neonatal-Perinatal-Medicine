<details><summary>Which embryologic tissues form the early alimentary canal?</summary>

Endoderm and splanchnic mesoderm. Folding of endoderm and splanchnic mesoderm produces the early gut tube.

<details><summary>Why is esophageal blood supply described as segmental?</summary>

Different esophageal regions receive distinct arterial branches. Segmental arterial inputs matter when preserving perfusion during mobilization.

PDF p. 1596
</details>

<details><summary>How does normal intestinal fixation reduce the risk of midgut volvulus?</summary>

It creates a broad mesenteric base that resists twisting. Widely separated fixation points broaden the mesenteric base and reduce twisting risk.

PDF p. 1599
</details>

<details><summary>What is the major digestive and absorptive portion of the gastrointestinal tract?</summary>

Small intestine. The small intestine is the major digestive and absorptive region; villi and microvilli greatly increase surface area.

</details>

<details><summary>What are the two basic functions of the colon described in the chapter?</summary>

Water-electrolyte absorption and storage/elimination of feces. The colon absorbs water and electrolytes and stores and eliminates feces.

PDF p. 1601
</details>

<details><summary>Why can a normally positioned colon fail to exclude abnormal duodenal rotation?</summary>

Duodenal and colonic rotation are not completely codependent; one can be abnormal while the other appears appropriately rotated.
