<details><summary>What is the most common cause of infant stridor in this source?</summary>

Laryngomalacia. Laryngomalacia produces dynamic supraglottic collapse and is the most common infant stridor cause; severe symptoms require further assessment.

<details><summary>What findings can accompany unilateral vocal-cord paralysis?</summary>

Unilateral vocal-cord paralysis with possible aspiration. Vocal-cord dysfunction can cause dysphonia and feeding-related aspiration symptoms; laryngoscopy can confirm mobility.

Subglottic infantile hemangioma. Segmental beard-distribution hemangioma can be associated with a subglottic vascular lesion requiring airway evaluation.

<details><summary>Why can formal operative assessment be needed for a laryngeal cleft?</summary>

Formal airway evaluation with palpation of the interarytenoid region. Swallow assessment and flexible laryngoscopy contribute, but definitive diagnosis requires formal evaluation and palpation.
