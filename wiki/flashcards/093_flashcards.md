<details><summary>Which fetal signal induces müllerian duct regression?</summary>

Müllerian duct structures. AMH acts locally to induce müllerian regression; testosterone instead supports wolffian differentiation.

<details><summary>How is dihydrotestosterone produced for external genital differentiation?</summary>

5-alpha-reductase conversion to dihydrotestosterone. 5-alpha-reductase generates DHT from testosterone in peripheral tissues; DHT is the potent androgen emphasized for external differentiation.

PDF p. 1754
</details>

<details><summary>What elements form the initial evaluation of a newborn with suspected DSD?</summary>

Chromosome assessment, pelvic/renal imaging and relevant adrenal hormones/electrolytes. First-tier evaluation integrates chromosomes, anatomy and biochemistry; gonadal findings help direct hormone studies.

PDF p. 1760
</details>

<details><summary>Why must 17-hydroxyprogesterone and testosterone be interpreted by age and illness status?</summary>

Prematurity, illness and neonatal hormone changes can affect the result. The source notes elevated 17-OHP in premature or sick infants and physiologic testosterone surges; results need contextual confirmation.

PDF p. 1761
</details>

<details><summary>What should early discussion with the family include?</summary>

A private, calm discussion explaining findings, consultations, uncertainty and expected timing. Experienced clinicians should provide privacy, explain the evaluation and support bonding while information develops.

PDF p. 1759
</details>

<details><summary>Which fetal hormone supports development of wolffian ducts?</summary>

Locally produced testosterone supports ipsilateral wolffian duct differentiation; this role differs from AMH-driven müllerian regression and DHT-dependent external differentiation.
