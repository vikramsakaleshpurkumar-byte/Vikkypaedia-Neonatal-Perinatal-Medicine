<details><summary>What gestational ages define late preterm birth?</summary>

Late preterm. Late preterm spans 34 weeks through 36 weeks 6 days; size does not establish maturity.

</details>

<details><summary>Why can late preterm infants struggle to transfer milk?</summary>

Immature suck-swallow-breathe coordination and endurance. Neurologic and oral-motor immaturity can make feeding ineffective despite mature appearance.

PDF p. 718
</details>

<details><summary>What clearance process contributes to respiratory transition after birth?</summary>

Immaturity of epithelial sodium-mediated fluid absorption. The chapter links epithelial sodium transport, labor-related signals and maturation to lung liquid clearance.

PDF p. 719
</details>

<details><summary>Why do late preterm infants need careful bilirubin follow-up?</summary>

Increased risk of important hyperbilirubinemia after discharge. Late preterm bilirubin handling and feeding vulnerabilities increase jaundice and readmission risk.

PDF p. 723
</details>

<details><summary>What feeding evidence is sought before discharge in the source pathway?</summary>

Continue assessment until successful feeding is documented. The pathway requires successful feeding with coordinated sucking, swallowing and breathing, alongside other readiness criteria.

PDF p. 725
</details>

<details><summary>What follow-up interval does the source recommend after late preterm discharge?</summary>

Arrange a physician visit within 24–48 hours after discharge, with feeding, jaundice and caregiver readiness assessed in the discharge plan.

PDF p. 726
</details>
