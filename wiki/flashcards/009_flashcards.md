<details><summary>What family pattern commonly characterizes autosomal recessive disease?</summary>

Horizontal transmission among siblings. Two pathogenic alleles can produce disease in siblings of unaffected heterozygous parents.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the origin of the fetal-associated DNA signal used in maternal cfDNA screening?</summary>

Placental trophoblast. The detectable fetal-associated DNA in maternal blood is of placental origin.

PDF p. 177
</details>

<details><summary>What tissue is sampled by chorionic villus sampling?</summary>

Placental villi. CVS obtains placental tissue, usually at 10–13 weeks, for genetic diagnosis.

PDF p. 181
</details>

<details><summary>What major diagnostic advantage does microarray offer over routine karyotyping?</summary>

Chromosomal microarray for small copy-number changes. Microarrays detect smaller copy-number gains and losses than routine karyotypes.

PDF p. 183
</details>

<details><summary>How should pregnancy affect decisions about necessary diagnostic imaging?</summary>

Balance exposure risk against the risk of delayed diagnosis. Appropriate test selection and modification reduce exposure, while delayed diagnosis can also harm mother and fetus.

PDF p. 174
</details>

<details><summary>What distinction should be maintained between a screening result and a genetic diagnosis?</summary>

Screening estimates risk; diagnostic modalities examine appropriate fetal or placental samples to establish the tested genetic finding.
