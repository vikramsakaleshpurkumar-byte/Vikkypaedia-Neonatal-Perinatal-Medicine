Pregestational diabetes. Pregestational diabetes precedes conception; gestational diabetes is recognized during pregnancy.

<details><summary>What limitation of risk-factor-only GDM screening does the source describe?</summary>

Many affected patients have no identified risk factor. The source describes substantial missed cases among women without historical risk factors.

<details><summary>Why does glucose control before conception matter for anomaly prevention?</summary>

Organogenesis occurs early, often before later pregnancy treatment begins. The relevant malformation window is early organogenesis, so preconception control is important.

PDF p. 325
</details>

<details><summary>Why can an infant of a diabetic mother become hypoglycemic after delivery?</summary>

Persistent increased insulin after maternal glucose supply stops. Insulin production remains upregulated after separation from the maternal nutrient source.

PDF p. 328
</details>

<details><summary>Why does a history of GDM require postpartum follow-up?</summary>

Future diabetes risk remains and glucose reassessment is needed. Insulin needs often fall postpartum, but impaired glucose metabolism and later type 2 diabetes remain important risks.

PDF p. 327
</details>

<details><summary>What symptoms can accompany hypoglycemia in infants of diabetic mothers?</summary>

Infants may be asymptomatic or have tremor, seizures, poor feeding, respiratory distress or hypotonia, supporting glucose monitoring rather than reliance on symptoms alone.
