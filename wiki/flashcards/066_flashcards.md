<details><summary>How does CPAP improve respiratory mechanics in an immature lung?</summary>

Maintenance of end-expiratory lung volume and airway patency. CPAP prevents end-expiratory collapse, supports FRC and can stent the upper airway.

PDF p. 1242
</details>

<details><summary>What is the purpose of hybrid volume-targeted ventilation?</summary>

Keep tidal volume within a target range despite changing mechanics. Hybrid systems use feedback to adjust delivery toward a volume target; neonatal leaks and measurement limitations still matter.

PDF p. 1253
</details>

<details><summary>Why can a short expiratory time cause inadvertent PEEP?</summary>

Gas trapping with incomplete exhalation. Insufficient time relative to the resistance-compliance time constant leaves gas behind and can cause overinflation.

PDF p. 1250
</details>

<details><summary>What ventilated-infant pattern suggests tension pneumothorax?</summary>

Tension pneumothorax. Pleural air under pressure can impair ventilation and circulation and requires prompt treatment.

PDF p. 1266
</details>

<details><summary>How do volutrauma and atelectrauma differ?</summary>

Volutrauma and atelectrauma, respectively. Volutrauma is overdistention injury; atelectrauma follows repetitive recruitment and derecruitment.

PDF p. 1267
</details>

<details><summary>Why must support be reassessed when lung compliance improves?</summary>

A given pressure can deliver a larger tidal volume as compliance improves; volume and gas-exchange monitoring help avoid unintended overventilation and overdistention.

PDF p. 1253
</details>
