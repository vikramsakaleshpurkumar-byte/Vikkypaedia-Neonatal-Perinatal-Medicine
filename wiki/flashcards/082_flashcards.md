<details><summary>How should neutropenia be defined in a neonate?</summary>

An appropriate neonatal age and gestation reference distribution. Neonatal neutrophil values change rapidly and definitions depend on the relevant reference distribution.

</details>

<details><summary>What defect underlies chronic granulomatous disease?</summary>

NADPH oxidase dysfunction. CGD impairs oxidative microbial killing through defective NADPH oxidase in phagocytes.

PDF p. 1558
</details>

<details><summary>What pattern suggests leukocyte adhesion deficiency?</summary>

Impaired neutrophil adhesion and migration into tissues. LAD impairs trafficking; neutrophils remain in blood rather than accumulating effectively at infected tissues.

PDF p. 1559
</details>

<details><summary>Why is suspected severe combined immunodeficiency urgent?</summary>

Treat suspected severe combined immunodeficiency as an urgent specialist problem. SCID is described as a pediatric emergency; preserved B-cell numbers in some forms do not establish effective antibody function.

PDF p. 1560
</details>

<details><summary>Which constellation should raise suspicion for hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis?</summary>

Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis. HLH combines systemic inflammation, cytopenias and characteristic biochemical and immune abnormalities; neonatal cases are rare.

PDF p. 1563
</details>

<details><summary>Does a normal or increased neutrophil count exclude a neutrophil functional defect?</summary>

No. CGD and leukocyte adhesion defects may present with normal or increased circulating counts while killing or tissue migration is impaired.

</details>
