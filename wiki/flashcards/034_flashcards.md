<details><summary>How does delaying cord clamping change placental blood distribution?</summary>

More placental blood can return to the infant. Delay permits placental transfusion and reduces residual placental blood volume.

</details>

<details><summary>Which preterm benefits are supported for delayed cord clamping in the source?</summary>

Reduced hospital mortality and fewer blood transfusions. The source reports reduced mortality, higher hematocrit and reduced transfusion needs.

PDF p. 595
</details>

<details><summary>What later hematologic benefit is described after delayed clamping in term infants?</summary>

Iron-deficiency anemia. Placental transfusion improves early hemoglobin and hematocrit and reduces later infant iron-deficiency anemia.

PDF p. 595
</details>

<details><summary>How do intact and cut-cord milking differ?</summary>

Intact cord milking. Intact milking uses the attached uncut cord with repeated refilling; cut-cord milking uses a detached segment.

PDF p. 598
</details>

<details><summary>What risk signal limits cord milking in extremely preterm infants?</summary>

Higher severe IVH risk with milking than delayed clamping in extremely preterm infants. The source cites severe-IVH concerns in23–27-week infants and warns against extrapolating one strategy to another.

</details>

Many trials excluded these infants or did not separately report them, and possible transfusion benefit must be weighed against timely resuscitation.
