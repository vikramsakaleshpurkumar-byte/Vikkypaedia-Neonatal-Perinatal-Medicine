<details><summary>What does serum creatinine immediately after birth substantially reflect?</summary>

The initial value reflects maternal creatinine as well as neonatal transition. Immediately after birth, creatinine reflects the maternal concentration; the postnatal trend and gestation are important.

<details><summary>Why is bag-collected urine unsuitable for confirming a neonatal UTI?</summary>

Fecal contamination can cause false positives; obtain an appropriate culture specimen. A bag can provide urinalysis material but is not recommended for culture confirmation; catheterization is more reliable.

<details><summary>What does glycosuria with phosphate and amino-acid wasting suggest?</summary>

Generalized proximal tubular dysfunction. Combined urinary solute losses suggest a proximal tubulopathy such as Fanconi syndrome.

</details>

<details><summary>Why can prolonged renal hypoperfusion progress beyond a functional prerenal problem?</summary>

Acute tubular injury after a prolonged prerenal state. Prolonged hypoperfusion can lead to intrinsic tubular injury; perinatal illness and nephrotoxins are relevant risks.

</details>

<details><summary>Which study is diagnostic for posterior urethral valves?</summary>

Voiding cystourethrogram. VCUG demonstrates the posterior urethral obstruction and associated reflux; treatment centers on securing urinary drainage.

The metanephros forms the definitive kidney through reciprocal development of ureteric bud and metanephric mesenchyme.
