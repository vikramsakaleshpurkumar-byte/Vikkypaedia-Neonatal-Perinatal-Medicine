<details><summary>Why is EEG important even when no convulsions are visible?</summary>

Electrographic-only seizures can occur without visible signs. A large proportion of neonatal seizures are electrographic-only and require monitoring for recognition.

</details>

<details><summary>Which features favor jitteriness over clonic seizure?</summary>

Jitteriness, with assessment for underlying causes. Stimulus sensitivity and suppressibility favor jitteriness; hypoglycemia, hypocalcemia and other causes still need consideration.

PDF p. 1096
</details>

<details><summary>What pattern supports benign neonatal sleep myoclonus?</summary>

Benign neonatal sleep myoclonus. Sleep restriction, cessation with arousal and no ictal correlate support the benign sleep-related phenomenon.

PDF p. 1097
</details>

<details><summary>What is electroclinical dissociation after treatment?</summary>

Suppression of visible activity without termination of electrical seizures. Antiseizure medication can suppress the motor component while cortical electrical activity persists.

PDF p. 1096
</details>

<details><summary>How should adequacy of neonatal antiseizure treatment be assessed?</summary>

EEG assessment of electrical seizure activity. The source identifies elimination of electrical seizure activity as the therapeutic endpoint because clinical observation is unreliable.

PDF p. 1108
</details>

<details><summary>Why should neonatal seizure evaluation investigate etiology as well as treat events?</summary>

Hypoxic-ischemic injury, stroke, hemorrhage, infection and metabolic disturbances can require specific investigation and treatment; an antiseizure response does not identify the cause.

</details>
