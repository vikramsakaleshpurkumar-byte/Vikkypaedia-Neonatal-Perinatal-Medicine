Prechtl General Movement Assessment for neuromotor risk. General movement quality can help identify risk of neuromotor deficits, including spastic cerebral palsy.

<details><summary>Why can early infant testing underestimate later difficulties?</summary>

Higher-level demands can expose difficulties not captured by early developmental tests. Early assessments emphasize different skills; subtle cognitive, behavioral and coordination problems may emerge later.

PDF p. 1182
</details>

<details><summary>Why compare participants lost to follow-up with those retained?</summary>

Attrition may bias the observed outcome rates. Differential dropout can change the apparent risk and limits generalizability.

PDF p. 1182
</details>

<details><summary>Why should a positive autism screen in a preterm child receive contextual evaluation?</summary>

Further evaluation should consider coexisting impairments and the full developmental profile. The source notes substantial coexisting disability among screen-positive preterm children; screening supports evaluation rather than replacing it.

PDF p. 1184
</details>

Parent education and relationships, infant development, and individualized physical, occupational, speech or educational therapies according to identified needs.
