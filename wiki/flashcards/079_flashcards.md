<details><summary>What is the rationale for early prostaglandin E1 when ductal-dependent disease is suspected?</summary>

Promptly maintain ductal patency with prostaglandin E1 under monitoring. The chapter recommends prompt PGE1 while the diagnosis is confirmed because ductal flow may be lifesaving.

<details><summary>What is balloon atrial septostomy intended to improve?</summary>

Balloon atrial septostomy. Septostomy enlarges the atrial communication to improve mixing or atrial egress as required by the anatomy.

<details><summary>When can pulmonary artery banding be useful palliation?</summary>

Restrict pulmonary flow to improve circulatory balance. Banding is used selectively to limit pulmonary overcirculation, including some single-ventricle pathways.

PDF p. 1481
</details>

<details><summary>Why can oxygen and ventilation strategies require special care in ductal-dependent single-ventricle physiology?</summary>

Changing pulmonary resistance can redistribute flow away from systemic perfusion. Single-ventricle output must supply both beds; lowering pulmonary resistance may increase pulmonary flow at the expense of systemic delivery.

PDF p. 1491
</details>

<details><summary>Which effects of milrinone may assist a dysfunctional neonatal ventricle?</summary>

Inotropic, lusitropic and afterload-reducing effects. The combined effects can improve filling, stroke volume and output while reducing afterload.

PDF p. 1493
</details>

<details><summary>Does most mild or moderate congenital heart disease require a special neonatal ventilation strategy?</summary>

Often standard neonatal strategies appropriate to age and size suffice; special resistance-balancing strategies depend on the specific physiology.
