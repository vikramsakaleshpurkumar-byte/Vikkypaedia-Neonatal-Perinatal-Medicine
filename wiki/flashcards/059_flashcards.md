<details><summary>How should head circumference be measured and interpreted?</summary>

Measure maximal occipitofrontal circumference and plot on an age-appropriate preterm chart. Consistent occipitofrontal measurement and age-adjusted growth charts help assess intracranial growth.

<details><summary>What makes a full anterior fontanelle more concerning?</summary>

Consider an intracranial process with increased pressure. Sustained bulging can indicate raised intracranial pressure; crying-related transient fullness is different.

</details>

<details><summary>Does macrocephaly always mean hydrocephalus?</summary>

Enlarged brain, abnormal intracranial structures or hydrocephalus. The chapter distinguishes increased brain volume, masses or vascular lesions, and enlarged CSF spaces.

PDF p. 1138
</details>

<details><summary>How does a fused cranial suture alter skull growth?</summary>

Restricted growth perpendicular to the fused suture with compensatory growth elsewhere. Failure of perpendicular growth and compensation at unfused sites produce characteristic patterns.

PDF p. 1143
</details>

<details><summary>Which ear displacement favors positional plagiocephaly?</summary>

Positional plagiocephaly. Forward ipsilateral ear displacement and a parallelogram pattern favor positional deformity; lambdoid synostosis tends to displace the ear posteriorly.

PDF p. 1145
</details>

<details><summary>Why should head assessment include both size and shape?</summary>

Circumference tracks intracranial growth, while shape and suture examination can reveal positional deformation or premature suture fusion even without a striking size abnormality.
