<details><summary>What does a low stool osmotic gap suggest?</summary>

Electrolyte transport-related diarrhea. A low gap supports electrolyte transport-related diarrhea; unabsorbed dietary solutes tend to produce a high gap.

<details><summary>Why may fecal elastase be falsely low in watery diarrhea?</summary>

Dilution can lower the measured concentration. Watery diarrhea can dilute fecal elastase; maturation and clinical context also affect interpretation.

</details>

<details><summary>What acid-base pattern can occur in congenital chloride diarrhea?</summary>

Hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis. Loss of chloride and sodium through defective intestinal chloride-bicarbonate exchange can cause volume contraction and metabolic alkalosis.

PDF p. 1617
</details>

<details><summary>Why can breast milk and lactose-containing formula both provoke glucose-galactose malabsorption symptoms?</summary>

Defective SGLT1-mediated glucose and galactose absorption. Lactose digestion releases glucose and galactose; failure of their transport leaves osmoles in the lumen.

</details>

<details><summary>What pattern suggests an inherited intestinal zinc absorption defect?</summary>

Zinc deficiency from acrodermatitis enteropathica. SLC39A4-related zinc malabsorption causes the characteristic dermatitis and diarrhea; supplementation is required long term.

PDF p. 1618
</details>

<details><summary>How is the stool osmotic gap calculated in the chapter?</summary>

Estimated gap = 290 − 2 × (stool sodium + stool potassium), using compatible concentration units; low and high gaps support different mechanisms and are interpreted clinically.
