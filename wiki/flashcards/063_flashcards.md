<details><summary>During which stage does most airway branching occur?</summary>

Pseudoglandular stage. The pseudoglandular stage establishes the branching airway pattern and associated vascular pathways.

<details><summary>Which changes make gas exchange increasingly possible in the canalicular lung?</summary>

Acinar formation, thinning air-blood barrier and emerging type II-cell surfactant synthesis. Airspace and vascular approximation plus epithelial differentiation begin to establish gas-exchange capacity.

PDF p. 1193
</details>

<details><summary>What do lamellar bodies in developing type II cells signify?</summary>

Surfactant storage and secretion. Lamellar bodies are characteristic organelles of surfactant-producing type II epithelial cells.

PDF p. 1195
</details>

<details><summary>What epithelial transport helps clear lung liquid after birth?</summary>

Sodium absorption through epithelial sodium channels. Active sodium transport promotes absorption; developmental and labor-related signals influence the transition from secretion to clearance.

PDF p. 1198
</details>

<details><summary>How does surfactant support alveolar stability?</summary>

Higher surface tension promotes collapse and heterogeneous expansion. Low surface tension helps prevent alveolar collapse and fluid entry; deficiency produces uneven recruitment and overdistention.

PDF p. 1204
</details>

<details><summary>What developmental pattern can chronic preterm lung injury produce?</summary>

Interrupted alveolarization and vascular development can yield fewer, larger alveoli and attenuated microvasculature, with less fibrosis than the classic older BPD pattern.

</details>
