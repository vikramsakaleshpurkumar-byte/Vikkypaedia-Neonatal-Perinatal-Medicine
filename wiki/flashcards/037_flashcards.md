<details><summary>Why should repeated ECG probe applications be minimized in very immature infants?</summary>

Skin injury and increased transepidermal water loss. Repeated applications can impair adhesion and signal and injure fragile skin, increasing water loss.

</details>

<details><summary>What happens if an arterial pressure transducer is too high relative to the heart?</summary>

An erroneously low blood-pressure reading. Incorrect elevation can lower the displayed arterial pressure; proper leveling and zeroing are required.

PDF p. 632
</details>

<details><summary>What feature helps pulse oximetry estimate arterial saturation?</summary>

Heartbeat-related pulsatile light absorption. Ratios of pulsatile to constant red and infrared signals estimate the fraction of oxygenated arterial hemoglobin.

PDF p. 634
</details>

<details><summary>Why can sidestream capnography underestimate CO2 in very small preterm infants?</summary>

Constant aspiration of expired gas relative to small available volumes. Sampling requirements can be problematic at very low tidal volumes; microstream approaches use smaller samples.

PDF p. 637
</details>

<details><summary>Why should transcutaneous CO2 values be interpreted with awareness of arterial agreement?</summary>

The relationship varies with perfusion, skin metabolism, electrode temperature and CO2 level, so displayed values are not universally interchangeable with arterial measurements.
