<details><summary>Why must fetal surveillance results be interpreted in clinical context?</summary>

False-positive results are possible and clinical context matters. Tests reflect biophysical correlates of hypoxemia and have false-positive results; not all causes of stillbirth are predictable.

<details><summary>What acceleration pattern defines a reactive NST in the described standard criterion?</summary>

At least two accelerations of 15 beats/min lasting at least 15 seconds. The described reactive NST has at least two accelerations peaking 15 beats/min above baseline and lasting at least 15 seconds.

PDF p. 230
</details>

<details><summary>How are individual biophysical profile components scored?</summary>

Each component receives either 0 or 2 points. The five components are assigned 0 or 2 points, yielding an even total between 0 and 10.

PDF p. 231
</details>

<details><summary>What AFI threshold defines oligohydramnios in this source?</summary>

Oligohydramnios. The source defines oligohydramnios by AFI of 5 cm or less, or no single vertical pocket greater than 2 cm.

PDF p. 232
</details>

<details><summary>How can oxytocin-associated tachysystole contribute to an abnormal fetal heart pattern?</summary>

Reduce or discontinue oxytocin as clinically indicated. Resolving tachysystole can improve fetal status; excessive contraction frequency limits recovery.

PDF p. 240
</details>

<details><summary>What should usually follow a maternal report of decreased fetal movement?</summary>

Clinical evaluation is usually appropriate; the source does not identify one optimal formal kick-count strategy or clear prevention benefit from routine quantified counting.
