<details><summary>Why is there an early therapeutic window after hypoxic-ischemic injury?</summary>

Secondary mitochondrial energy failure and delayed cell injury can follow the initial insult. The source describes delayed energy failure after the initial insult, supporting early assessment and intervention.

</details>

<details><summary>What is the role of modified Sarnat assessment early after birth?</summary>

Structured grading of encephalopathy using the modified Sarnat approach. Modified clinical scoring supports assessment within the early cooling decision window.

PDF p. 1063
</details>

<details><summary>Within what postnatal window were infants cooled in the cited trials?</summary>

Initiation within 6 hours of birth. The cited trials began hypothermia within 6 hours and demonstrated reduced death or severe disability in eligible mature infants.

PDF p. 1082
</details>

<details><summary>Which seizures may be missed by limited-channel aEEG?</summary>

Brief, low-amplitude or focal seizures may be missed. The chapter supports raw EEG review and conventional EEG because limited-channel displays can miss events.

PDF p. 1072
</details>

<details><summary>Why should one early Apgar score not be used alone for long-term prognosis?</summary>

Integrate serial examination, EEG, imaging and treatment context. Early Apgar scores have limited long-term predictive sensitivity; hypothermia also changes interpretation of older prognostic data.

PDF p. 1084
</details>

<details><summary>Why monitor and correct glucose disturbances after an asphyxial insult?</summary>

Hypoglycemia can add to injury in an already vulnerable brain; metabolic support is part of systemic and brain-oriented care.

</details>
