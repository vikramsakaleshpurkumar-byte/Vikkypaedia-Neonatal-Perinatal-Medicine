<details><summary>What differentiates central and obstructive apnea?</summary>

Obstructive apnea. Obstruction preserves effort against a closed airway; central apnea has absent respiratory effort.

PDF p. 1309
</details>

<details><summary>Why can impedance monitoring miss obstructive events?</summary>

Chest movement does not establish airway patency or ventilation. Impedance monitoring detects movement and central pauses but can miss obstruction while effort continues.

PDF p. 1309
</details>

<details><summary>What should a new increase in baseline apnea prompt?</summary>

Investigate infection, NEC and other new causes. New or increased apnea can signal sepsis or NEC and requires contextual assessment.

PDF p. 1313
</details>

<details><summary>Should acid suppression routinely treat apnea of prematurity?</summary>

Do not routinely use acid suppression to treat apnea. Temporal linkage is uncommon and routine reflux treatment has not shown apnea benefit; acid suppression can add infectious and NEC risks.

PDF p. 1313
</details>

<details><summary>Which receptor action contributes to caffeine’s respiratory stimulation?</summary>

Antagonism of adenosine receptors. Methylxanthines antagonize adenosine receptors and improve ventilation and responsiveness to carbon dioxide.

PDF p. 1314
</details>

<details><summary>How does the neonatal hypoxic ventilatory response differ from the sustained adult response?</summary>

It is often biphasic: an initial rise in ventilation is followed by depression, particularly in preterm infants.
