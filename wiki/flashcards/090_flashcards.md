<details><summary>Why can glucose measurements differ between plasma and whole blood?</summary>

Plasma and whole-blood values differ and delayed handling permits glycolysis. Whole-blood and plasma values differ; ongoing cellular glycolysis can lower an inadequately handled sample result.

An infant of a diabetic mother. Infants of diabetic mothers, growth abnormalities, prematurity and acute illness are among monitored risk groups.

<details><summary>Why is persistent hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia particularly hazardous?</summary>

Insulin restricts endogenous glucose release and alternative fuel availability. Excess insulin inhibits glycogenolysis and availability of alternate fuels, increasing the risk of brain injury.

</details>

<details><summary>Why can a preterm infant develop hyperglycemia despite a reasonable exogenous glucose infusion?</summary>

Persistent endogenous glucose production with immature insulin regulation. Prematurity, stress, endogenous production and altered insulin effects contribute; medications and sepsis may also be relevant.

</details>

<details><summary>How can activating KATP-channel mutations cause neonatal diabetes?</summary>

Failure of channel closure impairs insulin secretion. Activating channel mutations prevent normal closure and inhibit beta-cell insulin release.

</details>

<details><summary>What three tasks frame management of neonatal hypoglycemia?</summary>

Anticipate infants at risk, promptly correct low glucose and investigate and treat the underlying cause, especially when episodes persist or recur.
