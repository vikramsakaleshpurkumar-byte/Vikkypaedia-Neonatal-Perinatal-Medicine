<details><summary>Why is an adequate atrial communication important in dextro-transposition?</summary>

Parallel circulations require mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood. The two circuits run in parallel; atrial mixing is particularly effective and may be essential to adequate systemic oxygenation.

</details>

<details><summary>Where do pulmonary veins drain in total anomalous pulmonary venous connection?</summary>

Total anomalous pulmonary venous connection. All pulmonary venous blood returns anomalously to the systemic venous circulation; variants depend on the level of drainage.

PDF p. 1416
</details>

<details><summary>Why is systemic blood flow ductal dependent in hypoplastic left heart syndrome?</summary>

Loss of right-ventricular supply to the systemic circulation through the ductus. The right ventricle supplies systemic flow through the ductus, including retrograde arch and coronary flow; closure can cause profound hypoperfusion.

PDF p. 1419
</details>

<details><summary>What bedside findings should raise concern for critical aortic coarctation?</summary>

Critical aortic coarctation. Critical arch obstruction can present with systemic hypoperfusion as ductal support disappears; pulse differences are important clues.

PDF p. 1423
</details>

<details><summary>Why may a large ventricular septal defect become more symptomatic as pulmonary resistance falls?</summary>

Increasing left-to-right shunt as pulmonary resistance decreases. Falling pulmonary resistance promotes a larger left-to-right shunt and pulmonary overcirculation.

PDF p. 1425
</details>

<details><summary>Why must congenital heart disease be assessed by physiology as well as anatomic labels?</summary>

The same anatomic category can have different flow limitations and mixing; physiology changes with falling pulmonary resistance and closure of fetal channels.

</details>
