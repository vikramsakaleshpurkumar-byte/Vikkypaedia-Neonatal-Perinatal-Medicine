<details><summary>What pattern can congenital constriction bands produce?</summary>

Disruption associated with amniotic bands. Bands can constrict appendages, produce distal lymphedema and cause intrauterine amputations or syndactyly.

<details><summary>Why should renal ultrasound be considered in congenital scoliosis?</summary>

Renal ultrasound for genitourinary malformations. Genitourinary malformations, cardiac defects and spinal dysraphism may accompany congenital scoliosis; renal ultrasound assesses urinary anomalies.

</details>

<details><summary>How do Barlow and Ortolani maneuvers differ?</summary>

Barlow tests dislocatability; Ortolani assesses reduction. The maneuvers test different aspects of stability; a true shift or clunk must be distinguished from common benign clicks.

</details>

<details><summary>Why does a newborn with equinovarus require a broader musculoskeletal and neurologic examination?</summary>

Look for structural rigidity and associated neurologic or syndromic abnormalities. The chapter distinguishes congenital, teratologic and positional forms and recommends examination for other abnormalities.

</details>

<details><summary>What developmental errors underlie congenital scoliosis?</summary>

Failure of vertebral formation, failure of segmentation or mixed defects; their differing growth potential helps explain progression risk.
