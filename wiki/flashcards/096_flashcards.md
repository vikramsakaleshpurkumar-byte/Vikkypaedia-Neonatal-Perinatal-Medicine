<details><summary>What does serum sodium concentration represent compared with total-body sodium?</summary>

Concentration reflects sodium relative to water, while total sodium content influences extracellular volume. The concentration is a salt-water relationship; total sodium content is a major determinant of extracellular volume.

</details>

<details><summary>Why can very preterm infants have high insensible water losses?</summary>

Greater transepidermal evaporative loss through immature skin. Immature skin contributes substantially to evaporative loss; environment and treatments also influence requirements.

PDF p. 1944
</details>

<details><summary>What broad mechanisms can produce neonatal hyponatremia?</summary>

Water retention, excessive sodium loss or inadequate sodium intake. The chapter classifies hyponatremia through water handling, sodium losses and intake; clinical volume assessment separates them.

PDF p. 1947
</details>

<details><summary>What three questions organize acid-base interpretation?</summary>

Identify the primary abnormality, secondary compensation and whether compensation is appropriate. pH, bicarbonate and carbon dioxide together establish the primary process and adequacy of compensation; mixed disorders occur.

PDF p. 1953
</details>

<details><summary>Why can chloride and volume depletion maintain metabolic alkalosis?</summary>

Volume and chloride depletion favor bicarbonate retention and impaired alkali excretion. Reduced filtration and enhanced sodium-bicarbonate reabsorption, together with hormonal effects, help maintain alkalosis.

PDF p. 1954
</details>

<details><summary>Why are premature infants less able to compensate for an acute acid load?</summary>

Tubular immaturity limits bicarbonate conservation, urinary acidification and ammonium generation; low filtration also limits available urinary buffers.

PDF p. 1953
</details>
