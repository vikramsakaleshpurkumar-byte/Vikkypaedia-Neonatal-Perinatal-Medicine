## Authored MCQ

A premature infant has less maternally acquired antibody than a term infant. Which class is principally affected?

A. IgM
B. IgE
C. IgG
D. Secretory IgA

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

IgG

Placental Fc receptors actively transport maternal IgG, especially late in pregnancy.

## Authored MCQ

An infant evaluated for congenital infection has increased cord IgM. Which interpretation is supported?

A. Proof that all passive immunity is absent
B. Definitive identification of a particular organism by total IgM
C. Fetal antigen exposure and antibody synthesis, while considering maternal-fetal bleeding
D. Normal selective placental transfer of maternal IgM

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Fetal antigen exposure and antibody synthesis, while considering maternal-fetal bleeding

IgM does not normally cross placenta; elevation can reflect fetal synthesis, but total IgM is not pathogen-specific and bleeding can complicate interpretation.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A newborn encounters an organism before developing specific antibody. Which pathways can still initiate complement activation?

A. Only passive transfer of maternal complement
B. Alternative and lectin pathways
C. Only the classic antibody-dependent pathway
D. Only IgE-mediated mast-cell activation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Alternative and lectin pathways

Alternative activation can occur on surfaces and lectin activation follows recognition of microbial carbohydrates.

## Authored MCQ

A teaching comparison distinguishes systemic placental antibody from digestion-resistant milk antibody. Which milk component is most relevant?

A. IgM transported by placental Fc receptors
B. Complement C9 as an immunoglobulin
C. Secretory IgA
D. Monomeric IgE as the principal milk antibody

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Secretory IgA

Secretory IgA predominates and its secretory component supports resistance to gastrointestinal digestion.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has recurrent significant fungal and bacterial infections with poor response and growth failure. What should be considered?

A. An immune defect alongside other predisposing conditions
B. Excluding immune disorders until adulthood
C. Normal immaturity as a sufficient explanation in every case
D. A single total IgG value as the complete investigation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

An immune defect alongside other predisposing conditions

Unusual, recurrent or incompletely treated infections and associated growth problems warrant investigation of host defenses.

</details>
