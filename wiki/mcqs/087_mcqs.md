## Authored MCQ

A newborn has severe watery diarrhea. Stool electrolytes account for most of the estimated stool osmolality, leaving a low osmotic gap. Which mechanism is favored?

A. A normal stool with no fluid loss
B. Electrolyte transport-related diarrhea
C. Diet-induced diarrhea from unabsorbed sugars
D. An isolated gastric outlet obstruction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Electrolyte transport-related diarrhea

A low gap supports electrolyte transport-related diarrhea; unabsorbed dietary solutes tend to produce a high gap.

## Authored MCQ

An infant with very watery stool has a low fecal elastase result. What caveat should guide interpretation?

A. Watery stool proves pancreatic agenesis
B. Elastase measures intestinal glucose transport directly
C. Dilution can lower the measured concentration
D. Neonatal age never affects elastase values

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Dilution can lower the measured concentration

Watery diarrhea can dilute fecal elastase; maturation and clinical context also affect interpretation.

## Authored MCQ

A baby had polyhydramnios and now has watery diarrhea from birth that persists without feeds, with high stool chloride. Which pattern fits?

A. Normal intravascular volume in every case
B. Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis as an obligatory finding
C. Hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis
D. Respiratory alkalosis without electrolyte loss

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis

Loss of chloride and sodium through defective intestinal chloride-bicarbonate exchange can cause volume contraction and metabolic alkalosis.

## Authored MCQ

Severe diarrhea begins with feeds and has reducing substances, acidic pH and a large osmotic gap. Removing glucose and galactose stops it. What mechanism fits?

A. An obligatory pancreatic lipase deficiency
B. Isolated failure to absorb fructose
C. Defective SGLT1-mediated glucose and galactose absorption
D. A distal chloride exchanger defect causing persistent fasting diarrhea

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Defective SGLT1-mediated glucose and galactose absorption

Lactose digestion releases glucose and galactose; failure of their transport leaves osmoles in the lumen.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant develops diarrhea with scaly erythematous lesions around the mouth and anus and poor growth. Which deficiency should be considered?

A. Zinc deficiency from acrodermatitis enteropathica
B. Isolated congenital lactase deficiency
C. Pancreatic insufficiency from cystic fibrosis
D. Glucose-galactose malabsorption

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Zinc deficiency from acrodermatitis enteropathica

SLC39A4-related zinc malabsorption causes the characteristic dermatitis and diarrhea; supplementation is required long term.
