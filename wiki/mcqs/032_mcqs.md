## Authored MCQ

During asphyxia, cellular ATP declines. Which downstream process contributes to injury?

A. Complete elimination of excitatory neurotransmitters
B. Failure of ion pumps with sodium and calcium accumulation
C. Guaranteed preservation of all membrane gradients
D. Protection from subsequent reperfusion injury

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Failure of ion pumps with sodium and calcium accumulation

ATP depletion impairs ion pumps, causing swelling, calcium accumulation and vulnerability to oxidative damage.

## Authored MCQ

Hypoxanthine accumulates during ischemia and xanthine oxidase is present when oxygen returns. What can follow?

A. Proof that oxygen should never be restored
B. Instant disappearance of every oxidant pathway
C. Exclusive repair without additional injury risk
D. Free-radical production during purine metabolism

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Free-radical production during purine metabolism

Reintroduced oxygen permits xanthine-oxidase metabolism that generates ROS, illustrating reoxygenation injury.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Which set of targets is vulnerable to excessive reactive species after asphyxia?

A. Only intact chromosomes with no other targets
B. Only maternal tissues outside the infant
C. Membrane lipids, proteins and nucleic acids
D. Only extracellular sodium

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Membrane lipids, proteins and nucleic acids

Reactive species can peroxidize lipids and damage proteins, enzymes and nucleic acids.
