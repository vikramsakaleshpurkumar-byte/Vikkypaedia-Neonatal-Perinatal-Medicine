## Authored MCQ

A delivery is considered low risk. Which staffing principle still applies?

A. An individual responsible for the infant and capable of initiating PPV
B. No trained neonatal responder unless risk is identified prenatally
C. Only someone able to record Apgar scores
D. Resuscitation equipment without personnel training

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

An individual responsible for the infant and capable of initiating PPV

Unexpected neonatal depression occurs; the source calls for a trained person whose primary responsibility is the infant.

## Authored MCQ

What transition must a newly born infant establish for independent oxygenation?

A. Permanent diversion of all blood away from lungs
B. Continued exclusive placental gas exchange indefinitely
C. Closure of the airway to retain lung liquid
D. Aerated lungs with increased pulmonary perfusion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Aerated lungs with increased pulmonary perfusion

The lungs replace the placenta as the primary gas-exchange organ and must receive pulmonary blood flow.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant cannot establish adequate ventilation immediately after birth. Which approach is appropriate?

A. Wait until the formal one-minute score is assigned
B. Record the score instead of correcting ventilation
C. Begin indicated support based on breathing and heart rate without waiting for Apgar scoring
D. Use a future five-minute score to decide present support

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Begin indicated support based on breathing and heart rate without waiting for Apgar scoring

Waiting for the score delays therapy; Apgar describes the infant’s state at specified times.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A term newborn is vigorous and breathing after birth through clear fluid. What airway approach is supported?

A. Routine deep pharyngeal stimulation in every infant
B. Posterior pharyngeal stimulation has no potential adverse effect
C. Suctioning is required before any observation of breathing
D. No routine suctioning is necessary

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

No routine suctioning is necessary

Routine suction is unnecessary in this setting; posterior pharyngeal stimulation may provoke bradycardia.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Drying and gentle stimulation fail to establish breathing. Which next priority follows the source?

A. Blow-by oxygen as a substitute for ventilation in apnea
B. Prompt positive-pressure ventilation
C. Progressively more vigorous stimulation for several minutes
D. Wait for spontaneous improvement without reassessment

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Prompt positive-pressure ventilation

Failure to respond should prompt PPV; ongoing ineffective stimulation delays ventilation and may worsen hypoxia.

PDF p. 554
</details>
