## Authored MCQ

Cervical dilation produces cramping visceral pain; later perineal distention produces more localized pain. Which sequence fits?

A. Only placental pain in both stages
B. Only neuropathic pain in both stages
C. Somatic predominance first, no pain second
D. Visceral predominance first, somatic predominance second

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Visceral predominance first, somatic predominance second

First-stage cervical and lower uterine distention is predominantly visceral; pelvic and perineal distention adds somatic pain in the second stage.

## Authored MCQ

A systemic sedative has a cerebral effect in the mother. Which assessment is appropriate?

A. Assume maternal brain effects prove no placental transfer
B. Assume every drug creates identical fetal exposure
C. Ignore dose because pregnancy prevents distribution
D. Consider placental transfer plus drug properties, dose and route

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Consider placental transfer plus drug properties, dose and route

Lipid barrier crossing suggests transfer potential, while pharmacokinetics, dose and administration affect actual fetal exposure.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Blood pressure falls after a neuraxial block. Which mechanism links the block to a fetal concern?

A. Only direct fetal injection can affect perfusion
B. Sympathetic blockade always increases vascular resistance
C. Sympathetic blockade causes vasodilation and may reduce uteroplacental perfusion
D. Hypotension guarantees increased placental perfusion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Sympathetic blockade causes vasodilation and may reduce uteroplacental perfusion

Local-anesthetic sympathetic blockade causes vasodilation; persistent hypotension can impair uteroplacental flow.

</details>

A. Pudendal block
B. A maternal cervical sympathetic block
C. A neonatal caudal block
D. A fetal brachial plexus block

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pudendal block may provide late-stage labor analgesia, particularly for forceps delivery.
