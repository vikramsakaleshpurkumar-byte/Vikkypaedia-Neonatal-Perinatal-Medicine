## Authored MCQ

A high-risk neonate appears motionless during repeated evolving EEG discharges. Which interpretation fits?

A. Only generalized convulsions qualify as neonatal seizures
B. Electrographic-only seizures can occur without visible signs
C. All EEG discharges must represent movement artifact
D. No movement means no seizure is possible

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Electrographic-only seizures can occur without visible signs

A large proportion of neonatal seizures are electrographic-only and require monitoring for recognition.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn’s tremor is stimulus-sensitive, stops with gentle passive limb flexion and has no ocular deviation. Which interpretation is favored?

A. A diagnosis of normality requiring no metabolic assessment
B. A clonic seizure established solely by rhythmicity
C. Benign sleep myoclonus despite the infant being awake
D. Jitteriness, with assessment for underlying causes

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Jitteriness, with assessment for underlying causes

Stimulus sensitivity and suppressibility favor jitteriness; hypoglycemia, hypocalcemia and other causes still need consideration.

## Authored MCQ

A well newborn has jerks only during sleep that stop abruptly on waking, with no ictal EEG correlate. Which diagnosis is most consistent?

A. Hyperekplexia defined by sleep alone
B. Pathologic myoclonus established despite a normal examination
C. Benign neonatal sleep myoclonus
D. Persistent electroclinical status epilepticus

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Benign neonatal sleep myoclonus

Sleep restriction, cessation with arousal and no ictal correlate support the benign sleep-related phenomenon.

## Authored MCQ

After phenobarbital, a newborn’s jerking ends but evolving EEG seizures continue. What has occurred?

A. Proof that every EEG discharge is benign
B. Suppression of visible activity without termination of electrical seizures
C. A reason to stop all EEG observation
D. Complete seizure control established by the motor response

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Suppression of visible activity without termination of electrical seizures

Antiseizure medication can suppress the motor component while cortical electrical activity persists.

The source identifies elimination of electrical seizure activity as the therapeutic endpoint because clinical observation is unreliable.
