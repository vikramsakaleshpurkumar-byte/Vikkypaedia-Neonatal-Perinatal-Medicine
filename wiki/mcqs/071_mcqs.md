## Authored MCQ

A former extremely preterm infant develops pulmonary hypertension. Which developmental substrate contributes?

A. Only transient nasal obstruction at birth
B. Reduced alveolar-capillary growth with vascular dysfunction and remodeling
C. Excess normal intra-acinar arteries preventing resistance
D. A guarantee of normal vascular structure after oxygen stops

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced alveolar-capillary growth with vascular dysfunction and remodeling

Reduced vessel number and abnormal growth interact with hypoxia, hypercarbia and other stressors to elevate vascular resistance.

## Authored MCQ

A symptomatic former preterm infant has no measurable TR jet on echocardiography. Which interpretation is appropriate?

A. No serial assessment can be useful
B. Pulmonary pressures are necessarily normal
C. All other right-heart measurements are irrelevant
D. Pulmonary hypertension is not excluded; assess other findings and the clinical picture

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pulmonary hypertension is not excluded; assess other findings and the clinical picture

The chapter notes that absent measurable TRJV does not rule out pulmonary hypertension; other signs and longitudinal evaluation matter.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A child born preterm has expiratory flow limitation without a strong allergic history. Which interpretation fits?

A. Only infants with BPD can have later flow limitation
B. Prematurity-related airway injury and altered development can contribute
C. Wheeze must be allergic asthma in every child
D. Absence of atopy excludes all airway hyperreactivity

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Prematurity-related airway injury and altered development can contribute

Preterm wheezing phenotypes can differ from term allergic asthma and occur with or without BPD.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An adult born very preterm has persistent airflow limitation. Which explanation should remain in the differential?

A. Neonatal support history alone identifies the exact adult diagnosis
B. All preterm lungs necessarily catch up completely by adolescence
C. Prematurity has no possible effect after childhood
D. Reduced lung growth and altered lifelong function after prematurity

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced lung growth and altered lifelong function after prematurity

Developmental lung impairment and subsequent exposures can influence the trajectory toward adult obstructive disease.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An intervention changes a neonatal BPD outcome but childhood wheezing is unmeasured. Which inference is justified?

A. The BPD result proves prevention of every later wheeze
B. Retention in long-term follow-up has no effect on evidence
C. No child without BPD can have later respiratory disease
D. Longer-term respiratory benefit still needs direct assessment

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Longer-term respiratory benefit still needs direct assessment

BPD or discharge support is not a sufficient surrogate for all postneonatal airway outcomes.
