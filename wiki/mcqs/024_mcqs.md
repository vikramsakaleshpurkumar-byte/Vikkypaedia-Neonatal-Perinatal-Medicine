## Authored MCQ

Which route most commonly introduces organisms into the intra-amniotic compartment?

A. Direct inheritance as a Mendelian allele
B. Ascension from the lower maternal genital tract
C. Only transplacental spread after maternal systemic infection
D. Mandatory fetal bloodstream infection before maternal exposure

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Ascension from the lower maternal genital tract

Most cases result from ascending bacteria; procedural and hematogenous routes occur less often.

## Authored MCQ

Placental examination shows inflammation, but standard amniotic culture is negative. Which explanation remains possible?

A. Proof that the pathology report is impossible
B. A diagnosis of a specific organism from histology alone
C. Fastidious microbes or sterile intra-amniotic inflammation
D. Guaranteed absence of any inflammatory process

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Fastidious microbes or sterile intra-amniotic inflammation

Negative cultures may reflect recovery limitations or sterile inflammation, so inflammation and infection are not identical labels.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Maternal fever and other inflammatory signs raise concern for infection. Which qualification is essential?

A. No clinical assessment is possible before pathology
B. Individual signs have variable sensitivity and generally low specificity
C. Fever uniquely proves bacterial invasion
D. Every elevated white count proves neonatal sepsis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Individual signs have variable sensitivity and generally low specificity

Clinical criteria are often used because definitive sampling is unavailable, but their specificity is limited.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Clinical chorioamnionitis is diagnosed and antibiotic treatment and delivery are planned. What does the source say about delivery route?

A. Infection alone does not justify cesarean delivery
B. Cesarean eliminates all maternal infection risk
C. Antibiotics remove every need to evaluate fetal status
D. Cesarean is required solely by the infection label

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Infection alone does not justify cesarean delivery

No neonatal benefit from cesarean solely for chorioamnionitis is established, and maternal surgical complications may increase.

PDF p. 434
</details>
