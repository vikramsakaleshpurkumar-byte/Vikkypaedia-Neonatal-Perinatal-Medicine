## Authored MCQ

A hypotonic newborn is also poorly perfused and unwell. Which initial approach is supported?

A. Exclude sepsis because the infant is hypotonic
B. Assess acute systemic illness, metabolic disturbance and medication exposure
C. Begin with muscle biopsy before stabilization
D. Assume a congenital myopathy from tone alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Assess acute systemic illness, metabolic disturbance and medication exposure

Many acute or multisystem illnesses and maternal medications can reduce tone.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has low tone but retains substantial antigravity limb movement. Which localization is generally favored, with acknowledged exceptions?

A. A complete spinal motor paralysis despite preserved power
B. Central hypotonia with less weakness than the degree of low tone
C. A peripheral motor lesion proven by low tone alone
D. No neurologic cause can produce this combination

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Central hypotonia with less weakness than the degree of low tone

Central causes often reduce tone disproportionately to weakness; exceptions require full assessment.

## Authored MCQ

An infant develops severe symmetric flaccid weakness, greater proximally and in the legs. Which localization fits?

A. Isolated sensory receptor dysfunction
B. Only cortical hyperexcitability
C. Anterior horn cell degeneration
D. Only a structural tongue lesion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Anterior horn cell degeneration

Infantile SMA affects spinal anterior horn cells and lower brainstem motor nuclei.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has profound hypotonia, a tent-shaped upper lip and respiratory weakness; the mother has subtle myotonic features. Which disorder is most consistent?

A. Isolated hypoglycemia as the only possible cause
B. A purely acquired peripheral nerve injury from delivery
C. Congenital myotonic dystrophy
D. Duchenne muscular dystrophy as the typical neonatal presentation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Congenital myotonic dystrophy

The congenital form of DM1 is usually maternally inherited; maternal examination and molecular testing support diagnosis.

## Authored MCQ

A weak hypotonic infant has macroglossia, cardiomegaly and a short PR interval. Which enzyme deficiency should be considered?

A. Acid alpha-glucosidase
B. Glucose-6-phosphatase as the defining Pompe defect
C. Galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase
D. Phenylalanine hydroxylase

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Acid alpha-glucosidase

Acid alpha-glucosidase deficiency produces lysosomal glycogen accumulation involving muscle and heart.
