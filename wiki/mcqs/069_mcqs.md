## Authored MCQ

A newborn becomes cyanotic while resting and feeding but improves when crying. Which lesion should be considered?

A. A benign normal respiratory pattern
B. Only distal alveolar surfactant deficiency
C. Bilateral choanal atresia
D. An isolated unilateral vocal-cord paresis as the characteristic explanation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Bilateral choanal atresia

Preferential nasal breathing makes bilateral posterior nasal obstruction particularly symptomatic; crying can allow oral airflow.

## Authored MCQ

An infant develops coarse inspiratory stridor in the first weeks, worse with agitation, with a usually normal cry. Which lesion is most consistent?

A. Vocal-cord paralysis with prominent dysphonia
B. Laryngomalacia
C. Subglottic hemangioma with progressive biphasic stridor
D. Bilateral choanal atresia with cry-relieved cyanosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Laryngomalacia

Laryngomalacia produces dynamic supraglottic collapse and is the most common infant stridor cause; severe symptoms require further assessment.

## Authored MCQ

After thoracic surgery, an infant has a weak cry and coughing or cyanosis with feeding. Which diagnosis deserves assessment?

A. Only transient tachypnea
B. Normal swallowing proven by a quiet chest
C. Unilateral vocal-cord paralysis with possible aspiration
D. Bilateral choanal atresia developing from surgery on the recurrent nerve

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Unilateral vocal-cord paralysis with possible aspiration

Vocal-cord dysfunction can cause dysphonia and feeding-related aspiration symptoms; laryngoscopy can confirm mobility.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has a cervicofacial hemangioma and progressive biphasic stridor beginning in early infancy. Which lesion should be investigated?

A. Laryngomalacia
B. Subglottic infantile hemangioma
C. Laryngeal cleft
D. Congenital subglottic stenosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Subglottic infantile hemangioma

Segmental beard-distribution hemangioma can be associated with a subglottic vascular lesion requiring airway evaluation.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has recurrent aspiration and a posterior laryngeal cleft is suspected after flexible examination. Which assessment establishes the anatomic diagnosis?

A. Only observing one feed without airway assessment
B. A negative skin swab
C. Formal airway evaluation with palpation of the interarytenoid region
D. A normal chest radiograph alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Formal airway evaluation with palpation of the interarytenoid region

Swallow assessment and flexible laryngoscopy contribute, but definitive diagnosis requires formal evaluation and palpation.
