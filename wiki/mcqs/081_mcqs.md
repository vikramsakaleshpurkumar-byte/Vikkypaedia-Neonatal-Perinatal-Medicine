## Authored MCQ

A healthy term infant develops the expected gradual fall in hemoglobin over the first months. Which mechanism contributes?

A. A permanent loss of all erythroid progenitors
B. A switch to placental oxygen delivery after birth
C. A mandatory maternal antibody attack on every infant
D. Higher tissue oxygenation suppresses erythropoietin production

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Higher tissue oxygenation suppresses erythropoietin production

Improved postnatal oxygenation lowers EPO and erythropoiesis, contributing to physiologic anemia.

## Authored MCQ

An anemic infant has indirect hyperbilirubinemia, elevated LDH for age and reticulocytosis. Which mechanism is most consistent?

A. Accelerated red-cell destruction
B. A low hemoglobin with no need to assess gestation
C. Pure hemodilution without red-cell injury
D. Isolated deficient erythropoiesis as the only process

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Accelerated red-cell destruction

Hemolysis commonly increases indirect bilirubin and LDH and stimulates reticulocytes; concurrent marrow suppression can blunt that response.

## Authored MCQ

A maternal acid-elution test suggests fetal cells, but the mother has a disorder that increases her own Hb F. What limitation matters?

A. Any positive screen gives an exact hemorrhage volume without confirmation
B. Maternal Hb F cannot affect acid resistance
C. Maternal Hb F-containing cells can produce a false-positive result
D. The assay identifies fetal cells solely by their chromosome number

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Maternal Hb F-containing cells can produce a false-positive result

The assay exploits acid resistance of Hb F-containing cells, so elevated maternal Hb F can confound results; flow cytometry may distinguish populations.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A heel-stick hematocrit is elevated and a management decision is being considered. What distinction should be checked?

A. Capillary values tend to exceed peripheral venous values
B. A capillary result directly measures viscosity
C. Clinical symptoms never matter once hematocrit is known
D. Capillary and venous values are always identical

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Capillary values tend to exceed peripheral venous values

The chapter defines term polycythemia using peripheral venous values and notes higher capillary values; viscosity and clinical manifestations also matter.
