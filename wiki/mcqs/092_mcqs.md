A clinically quiet newborn has an abnormal thyroid screen. Why should timely confirmation and follow-up be prioritized?

A. The condition can safely be ignored until growth failure develops
B. Screening establishes every cause without confirmation
C. The absence of obvious symptoms excludes meaningful deficiency
D. Early detection and treatment can prevent neurodevelopmental disability

Early detection and treatment can prevent neurodevelopmental disability

The chapter emphasizes prevention of neurodevelopmental consequences through early recognition and treatment.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has low free T4 with a TSH that is not appropriately elevated. Which screening limitation is relevant?

A. A total T3 value alone reliably excludes central deficiency
B. Pituitary or hypothalamic deficiency may not produce high TSH
C. Every form of hypothyroidism causes the same large TSH rise
D. Normal TSH guarantees normal free T4

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pituitary or hypothalamic deficiency may not produce high TSH

Central hypothyroidism can have normal or only mildly elevated TSH; T4-based assessment is needed when suspected.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has a convincing abnormal thyroid result and confirmatory serum blood has been collected. Which approach matches the source?

A. Wait for all imaging before any replacement
B. Wait for overt developmental delay
C. Assume maternally acquired T4 persists indefinitely
D. Start replacement promptly rather than delaying for a complete etiologic workup

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Start replacement promptly rather than delaying for a complete etiologic workup

Treatment should begin promptly after confirmatory sampling; maternal T4 protection is temporary and delays can harm development.

## Authored MCQ

A mother previously treated definitively for Graves disease is now euthyroid, but her newborn develops hyperthyroidism. Which mechanism fits?

A. A requirement for maternal thyroid tissue to enter the fetal circulation
B. A requirement for the infant to produce autoantibodies before birth
C. Persistent maternal stimulating TSH-receptor antibodies crossing the placenta
D. A direct consequence of low thyroxine-binding globulin alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Persistent maternal stimulating TSH-receptor antibodies crossing the placenta

Maternal receptor-stimulating antibodies may persist after treatment and cross the placenta.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A clinically euthyroid infant has an abnormal total T4 with normal free T4. Which explanation should be considered?

A. Proof of severe tissue hypothyroidism from total T4 alone
B. Proof of neonatal Graves disease from total T4 alone
C. An inherited thyroid-hormone binding protein abnormality
D. Exclusion of laboratory interpretation because free T4 is measured

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

An inherited thyroid-hormone binding protein abnormality

TBG excess or deficiency can change total hormone concentrations while free T4 remains normal.
