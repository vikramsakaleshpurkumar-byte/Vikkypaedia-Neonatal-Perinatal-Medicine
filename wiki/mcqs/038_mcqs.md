## Authored MCQ

Which set of tasks explains frequent radiography in neonatal intensive care?

A. Guaranteed detection of every congenital lesion
B. Replacement of all bedside assessment
C. Device position, acute thoracoabdominal disease and treatment response
D. Direct identification of every genetic variant

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Device position, acute thoracoabdominal disease and treatment response

Radiographs assess support-device placement, urgent conditions and response to interventions.

## Authored MCQ

An ultrasound target is obscured by overlying bowel gas or bone. Which limitation explains the problem?

A. Ultrasound cannot be performed at the bedside
B. Operator skill never affects images
C. Ultrasound requires ionizing radiation to penetrate tissues
D. Sound transmits poorly through air and calcified structures

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Sound transmits poorly through air and calcified structures

Air and calcified structures restrict sonographic windows; operator skill and probe choice also matter.

## Authored MCQ

A premature infant has respiratory distress and a radiograph with low lung volumes and fine diffuse granular opacity. Which condition best fits?

A. Surfactant-deficient RDS
B. An isolated clavicle fracture explaining diffuse opacity
C. A normal radiograph excluding pulmonary disease
D. Typical hyperinflated retained-fluid pattern alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Surfactant-deficient RDS

Alveolar collapse produces the characteristic low-volume granular or ground-glass appearance.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has tachypnea, streaky perihilar opacity, hyperinflation and small effusions, with improvement over24–48 hours. Which process is suggested?

A. Transient tachypnea from retained lung fluid
B. A permanently diagnostic pattern of bacterial pneumonia
C. Uniform severe low-volume surfactant deficiency
D. A finding that establishes heart failure regardless of heart size

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Transient tachypnea from retained lung fluid

TTN is a clinical diagnosis with nonspecific retained-fluid appearances and usually a benign resolving course.

## Authored MCQ

A neonate has bilious vomiting but a nondiagnostic abdominal film. Which next reasoning step is appropriate?

A. Assume a normal film establishes normal duodenal fixation
B. Exclude surgical disease because the film is normal
C. Continue urgent evaluation for malrotation or volvulus when no other cause is defined
D. Treat the vomiting as physiologic without further assessment

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Continue urgent evaluation for malrotation or volvulus when no other cause is defined

Malrotation can have a normal radiograph; bilious vomiting is a potential surgical emergency requiring appropriate evaluation.
