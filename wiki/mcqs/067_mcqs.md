## Authored MCQ

A newborn has abdominal viscera in the thorax and severe pulmonary hypertension. Which underlying problem helps explain the severity?

A. A problem limited to the diaphragm’s cosmetic appearance
B. A normal contralateral lung and vasculature in every case
C. Pulmonary hypoplasia with abnormal vascular development
D. Only retained airway secretions with no developmental effect

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pulmonary hypoplasia with abnormal vascular development

Both lungs can have reduced airspace and vascular development, particularly on the involved side, with increased vascular resistance.

## Authored MCQ

An unfed newborn has a pleural effusion with a lymphatic origin suspected, but the fluid is clear. Which interpretation fits?

A. Pleural fluid analysis has no diagnostic role
B. All chylothoraces must be milky at birth
C. Clear fluid does not exclude chylothorax before enteral feeding
D. Clear pleural fluid is always air rather than liquid

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Clear fluid does not exclude chylothorax before enteral feeding

Chylous fluid becomes characteristically milky with established feeding; fluid analysis helps diagnosis.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A fetal echogenic lung mass has an arterial feeder from the systemic circulation and no normal bronchial communication. Which lesion is favored?

A. Uncomplicated diffuse surfactant deficiency
B. An isolated pneumothorax
C. Bronchopulmonary sequestration
D. Pure transient tachypnea

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Bronchopulmonary sequestration

Systemic arterial supply is a distinguishing feature of sequestrated nonfunctioning lung tissue.

## Authored MCQ

A term infant develops patchy atelectasis and low compliance after meconium aspiration. Which mechanism contributes?

A. Complete protection of alveoli from inflammatory mediators
B. Meconium invariably increases surfactant activity
C. Inflammatory injury and alveolar protein leak can damage type II cells and inactivate surfactant
D. Only a central respiratory pause without lung injury

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Inflammatory injury and alveolar protein leak can damage type II cells and inactivate surfactant

Aspiration injury combines obstruction, inflammation and impaired surfactant production or function.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant born by cesarean without labor has early tachypnea that resolves over 48–72 hours. Which mechanism is most consistent?

A. Complete irreversible alveolar destruction in every case
B. Delayed clearance of fetal lung liquid
C. Permanent bilateral choanal closure
D. Fixed pulmonary sequestration disappearing spontaneously in hours

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Delayed clearance of fetal lung liquid

Typical TTN reflects delayed liquid absorption and usually resolves over the first days; early pneumonia and other causes can overlap.

</details>
