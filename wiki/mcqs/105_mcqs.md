## Authored MCQ

A clinician considers applying a table labeled healthy term neonates to an extremely premature infant’s arterial sample at birth. What limitation matters?

A. The table concerns capillary samples from healthy term infants at about two days of age
B. Every neonatal table describes the same sample type
C. Capillary and arterial populations are automatically interchangeable
D. Gestation and postnatal age cannot affect reference applicability

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The table concerns capillary samples from healthy term infants at about two days of age

The table specifies healthy term infants and samples collected at 48 ± 12 hours; population and specimen differences limit direct transfer.

## Authored MCQ

A report lists bilirubin as 10 mg/dL and another system uses micromoles/L. Using the table factor 17.104, what is the equivalent concentration?

A. 171.04 micromoles/L
B. 1000 micromoles/L
C. 17.104 micromoles/L
D. 0.5846 micromoles/L

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

171.04 micromoles/L

Multiply the mg/dL value by 17.104: 10 × 17.104 = 171.04 micromoles/L. This is a unit conversion, not a treatment threshold.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Using the appendix values, elemental calcium has atomic weight 40 and valence 2. How many milliequivalents are represented by 40 mg of elemental calcium?

A. 20 mEq
B. 1 mEq
C. 2 mEq
D. 80 mEq

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

2 mEq

mEq = mg × valence / atomic weight; 40 × 2 / 40 = 2. This calculation concerns elemental calcium, not a calcium-salt dose.

</details>
