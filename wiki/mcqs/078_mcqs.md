## Authored MCQ

A febrile newborn has upright sinus P waves, heart-rate variability and tachycardia proportional to clinical stress. Which approach best fits?

A. Automatically start a chronic antiarrhythmic
B. Identify and treat the underlying cause
C. Assume every fast rate is accessory-pathway reentry
D. Ignore possible infection or anemia

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Identify and treat the underlying cause

Sinus tachycardia is usually a response to conditions such as fever, infection or anemia; management targets the cause.

## Authored MCQ

A neonatal ECG shows a short PR interval, a delta wave and a widened QRS complex. Which mechanism best explains it?

A. A concealed pathway conducting only retrogradely
B. Isolated respiratory sinus arrhythmia
C. Complete atrioventricular block
D. Antegrade conduction over a manifest accessory pathway

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Antegrade conduction over a manifest accessory pathway

Early ventricular activation through a manifest pathway produces pre-excitation; a concealed retrograde-only pathway has no baseline delta wave.

## Authored MCQ

An ECG shows atrial activity around 400 per minute with only every second or fourth atrial impulse followed by a QRS. What explains the slower ventricular rate?

A. Decremental atrioventricular nodal conduction
B. A ventricular rate unrelated to conduction properties
C. An absent atrial reentry circuit
D. Every atrial impulse normally reaching the ventricle

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Decremental atrioventricular nodal conduction

The AV node limits conduction of the rapid atrial rhythm, often in 2:1, 3:1 or 4:1 patterns.

## Authored MCQ

Several beats show progressive PR prolongation followed by a nonconducted P wave; the next conducted PR is shorter. Which pattern is present?

A. Mobitz type II block with fixed conducted PR intervals
B. Complete block with no conducted sinus beats
C. First-degree block with every P wave conducted
D. Mobitz type I second-degree block

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Mobitz type I second-degree block

Progressive PR lengthening before a dropped beat is the Wenckebach pattern.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has a prolonged corrected QT interval while receiving several medicines. What additional evaluation is required?

A. Treat a seizure-like event as necessarily unrelated to rhythm
B. Exclude arrhythmic risk because the infant is asymptomatic
C. Assume every prolonged QT is inherited
D. Review QT-prolonging medicines and electrolyte disturbances

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Review QT-prolonging medicines and electrolyte disturbances

Medicines and hypocalcemia or hypokalemia may cause or exacerbate QT prolongation; both acquired and congenital forms can cause dangerous arrhythmias.

</details>
