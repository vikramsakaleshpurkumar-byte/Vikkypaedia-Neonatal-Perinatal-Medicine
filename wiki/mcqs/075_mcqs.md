## Authored MCQ

After delivery, oxygen tension rises and the placental supply of prostaglandin E2 is removed. What ductal response do these changes favor?

A. Maintenance of fetal ductal relaxation
B. Reversal toward persistent right-to-left flow in every infant
C. Ductal smooth-muscle constriction
D. Immediate fibrous obliteration without a functional phase

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Ductal smooth-muscle constriction

Oxygen promotes ductal contraction and falling PGE2 removes a relaxing influence; anatomic obliteration follows functional closure.

## Authored MCQ

A premature infant has a persistently open ductus while pulmonary resistance continues to fall. Which consequence is expected from a large shunt?

A. Increasing pulmonary flow and left-heart volume load with possible systemic hypoperfusion
B. Complete protection against pulmonary edema
C. Obligate right-to-left shunting independent of systemic resistance
D. Progressively less pulmonary flow solely because resistance falls

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increasing pulmonary flow and left-heart volume load with possible systemic hypoperfusion

A persistent ductus permits increasing left-to-right shunting, pulmonary overflow and increased left-heart work as pulmonary resistance falls.

## Authored MCQ

A premature infant has a murmur and worsening respiratory support. Which investigation can confirm ductal patency, characterize flow and assess left-heart loading?

A. Color Doppler echocardiography
B. Murmur intensity alone
C. Chest radiograph alone
D. A biomarker interpreted without imaging

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Color Doppler echocardiography

Echocardiography demonstrates patency, direction and velocity and evaluates pulmonary overflow and systemic hypoperfusion.

## Authored MCQ

An echocardiogram shows a large ductal shunt and absent or reversed diastolic flow in the descending aorta. Which interpretation is most appropriate?

A. A stand-alone mandate for one particular closure procedure
B. Evidence supporting systemic hypoperfusion from a large shunt
C. A finding that excludes hemodynamic significance
D. Proof that systemic flow is always adequate

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Evidence supporting systemic hypoperfusion from a large shunt

Absent or reversed systemic diastolic flow is one marker of systemic hypoperfusion, interpreted with the broader clinical and echocardiographic picture.
