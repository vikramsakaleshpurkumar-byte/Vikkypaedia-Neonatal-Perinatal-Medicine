## Authored MCQ

A 10-day-old has a sepsis-like illness, hepatitis and coagulopathy without vesicles. Which infection remains important to consider?

A. A benign cold excluded from systemic assessment
B. Congenital rubella diagnosed solely by the absent rash
C. Disseminated herpes simplex infection
D. Localized oral thrush as the full explanation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Disseminated herpes simplex infection

Disseminated HSV can involve liver and lungs and may occur without skin vesicles.

## Authored MCQ

A neonate has seizures and concern for herpes encephalitis. Which investigation most directly assesses viral DNA in CSF?

A. Blood PCR as the sole test defining disease extent
B. Maternal total IgG alone
C. Routine CSF viral culture assumed highly sensitive
D. CSF HSV PCR

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

CSF HSV PCR

PCR is especially valuable because CSF viral culture has low recovery; blood PCR alone does not classify disease extent.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn fails hearing screening. What testing window best distinguishes congenital CMV from perinatally acquired infection?

A. Identification within the first 21 days
B. A positive infant test after several months assumed congenital
C. Maternal IgG alone at any age
D. First testing at 6 months with no stored neonatal specimen

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Identification within the first 21 days

Early identification is necessary because later positivity may reflect perinatal acquisition.

## Authored MCQ

A term newborn’s mother is confirmed HBsAg positive. Which prevention approach is described?

A. Vaccine alone after waiting for symptoms
B. Hepatitis B vaccine plus HBIG at separate sites within 12 hours
C. No prophylaxis if the newborn examination is normal
D. HBIG alone without completing vaccination

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hepatitis B vaccine plus HBIG at separate sites within 12 hours

Active vaccine and passive immunoglobulin provide complementary protection after known maternal infection.

## Authored MCQ

A fetus develops severe anemia and hydrops after maternal parvovirus infection. Which mechanism explains the findings?

A. Isolated obstruction of fetal lymphatic drainage in every case
B. Maternal IgE crossing placenta and destroying all leukocytes
C. Enhanced erythropoiesis causing excessive red-cell mass
D. Infection of erythroid precursors causing impaired red-cell production

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Infection of erythroid precursors causing impaired red-cell production

Erythroid maturation arrest can cause profound anemia and high-output cardiac failure with hydrops.

</details>
