## Authored MCQ

A weakly basic drug crosses into milk and becomes more ionized. Which process explains its retention?

A. Ion trapping in milk that is slightly more acidic than plasma
B. Active renal secretion into maternal milk
C. Complete exclusion of all unionized molecules
D. Binding only to infant albumin before ingestion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Ion trapping in milk that is slightly more acidic than plasma

A unionized weak base can cross the membrane and become ionized in milk, limiting diffusion back.

## Authored MCQ

A lactation assessment compares the milk-derived infant dose with the maternal dose, each in mg/kg/day. Which measure is being calculated?

A. Relative infant dose expressed as a percentage
B. Maternal protein-binding percentage
C. Infant renal clearance in mL/min
D. Milk-to-plasma ratio from one simultaneous sample

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Relative infant dose expressed as a percentage

RID divides weight-adjusted infant dose by weight-adjusted maternal dose and expresses the ratio as percent.

## Authored MCQ

A drug appears in milk but is poorly absorbed orally. Which additional factor limits infant systemic exposure?

A. Maternal plasma protein binding after ingestion by the infant
B. Low infant oral bioavailability
C. Milk concentration alone fixes systemic exposure
D. A requirement that every milk drug be absorbed completely

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Low infant oral bioavailability

The ingested drug must be absorbed to cause systemic effects; local intestinal effects and infant-specific uncertainty still matter.

## Authored MCQ

A breastfed newborn becomes unusually sleepy while the mother is receiving postoperative opioid analgesia. What should be assessed?

A. Possible opioid-related sedation or lethargy
B. Proof the infant needs a higher maternal opioid dose
C. Assured safety because a reported RID is low
D. A normal finding that never warrants drug review

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Possible opioid-related sedation or lethargy

Infants can be sensitive to opioid exposure; selection, lowest effective dose, duration and clinical monitoring matter.
