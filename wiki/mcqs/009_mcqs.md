## Authored MCQ

Unaffected parents have two children with the same recessive disorder. Which inheritance pattern is most characteristic?

A. Horizontal transmission among siblings
B. Transmission exclusively through mothers
C. Transmission exclusively from fathers to sons
D. Affected individuals in every generation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Horizontal transmission among siblings

Two pathogenic alleles can produce disease in siblings of unaffected heterozygous parents.

## Authored MCQ

Maternal blood is collected for aneuploidy screening. Which tissue principally supplies the pregnancy-associated DNA signal?

A. Placental trophoblast
B. Fetal amniotic epithelial cells
C. Fetal hematopoietic cells as the principal source
D. Maternal leukocytes

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Placental trophoblast

The detectable fetal-associated DNA in maternal blood is of placental origin.

A patient seeks first-trimester genetic diagnosis and is offered CVS. What will be sampled?

A. Fetal urine directly from the bladder
B. Fetal spinal fluid
C. Maternal endometrial biopsy alone
D. Placental villi

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

CVS obtains placental tissue, usually at 10–13 weeks, for genetic diagnosis.

## Authored MCQ

A fetal anomaly raises concern for a small chromosomal deletion below routine karyotype resolution. Which test offers the relevant advantage?

A. A maternal serum protein measurement alone
B. Chromosomal microarray for small copy-number changes
C. Ultrasound as direct measurement of DNA dosage
D. Routine karyotype repeated with the same resolution

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Chromosomal microarray for small copy-number changes

Microarrays detect smaller copy-number gains and losses than routine karyotypes.

PDF p. 183
</details>

## Authored MCQ

A pregnant patient needs evaluation of a potentially serious maternal disorder. Which principle best guides imaging selection?

A. Balance exposure risk against the risk of delayed diagnosis
B. Consider fetal exposure but ignore maternal diagnostic benefit
C. Exclude every imaging modality in pregnancy
D. Assume every study delivers the same fetal dose

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Balance exposure risk against the risk of delayed diagnosis

Appropriate test selection and modification reduce exposure, while delayed diagnosis can also harm mother and fetus.

PDF p. 174
</details>
