## Authored MCQ

A newborn is bradycardic after birth. Which common underlying mechanism makes effective PPV the first central priority?

A. A requirement for epinephrine before lung inflation
B. A primary coronary thrombosis in most newborns
C. Asphyxia from inadequate gas exchange
D. Universal severe blood-volume depletion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Asphyxia from inadequate gas exchange

Asphyxia is the usual cause; establishing ventilation often avoids compressions and drugs.

## Authored MCQ

The heart rate remains below 60/min despite ventilation that inflates the lungs. Which escalation is indicated?

A. Routine feeds as the immediate corrective measure
B. Chest compressions coordinated with ventilation
C. Repeated stimulation instead of ventilation
D. Withhold all support until an Apgar score is assigned

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Chest compressions coordinated with ventilation

Persistent severe bradycardia despite effective lung inflation is the compression indication described.

## Authored MCQ

Profound asphyxia produces vasodilation and inadequate aortic diastolic pressure. What is the intended effect of a vasoconstricting pressor?

A. Replace all coordinated ventilation
B. Eliminate the need for oxygenated blood
C. Lower aortic diastolic pressure further
D. Raise aortic diastolic pressure to improve coronary perfusion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Raise aortic diastolic pressure to improve coronary perfusion

Coronary perfusion during CPR depends on the diastolic pressure gradient; epinephrine can improve that gradient.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A depressed newborn has poor perfusion after asphyxia. Which interpretation should guide assessment?

A. Myocardial dysfunction may cause shock without low circulating volume
B. Volume depletion can be diagnosed from apnea alone
C. Every asphyxiated newborn has major blood loss
D. Asphyxia excludes any cardiac dysfunction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Myocardial dysfunction may cause shock without low circulating volume

Most asphyxiated infants are not hypovolemic; reduced myocardial function and cardiac output can explain shock.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A resuscitated infant initially improves, then develops unequal breath sounds, hypotension and asymmetric chest expansion. Which complication is most concerning?

A. An uncomplicated caput succedaneum
B. Isolated hypoglycemia explaining unilateral chest findings
C. Pneumothorax impairing venous return
D. Normal improvement in FRC alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pneumothorax impairing venous return

A large pneumothorax can compress venous return, reduce output and cause hypotension and bradycardia.

</details>
