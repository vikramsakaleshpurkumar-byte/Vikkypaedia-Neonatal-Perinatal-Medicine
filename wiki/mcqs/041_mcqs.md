## Authored MCQ

A newborn is delivered at 35 weeks 4 days. Which gestational category applies?

A. Full term
B. Very preterm
C. Late preterm
D. Early term

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Late preterm

Late preterm spans 34 weeks through 36 weeks 6 days; size does not establish maturity.

## Authored MCQ

A 35-week infant tires at the breast and transfers little milk despite an apparently strong initial latch. Which developmental limitation is most relevant?

A. Fully mature feeding skills with no gestational effect
B. Excessive renal glucose excretion as the usual mechanism
C. Immature suck-swallow-breathe coordination and endurance
D. Increased intestinal capacity requiring fewer feeds

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Immature suck-swallow-breathe coordination and endurance

Neurologic and oral-motor immaturity can make feeding ineffective despite mature appearance.

## Authored MCQ

A late preterm infant delivered without labor develops tachypnea. Which mechanism can contribute to retained lung liquid?

A. Complete absence of fluid secretion throughout fetal life
B. Immaturity of epithelial sodium-mediated fluid absorption
C. Maturity of surfactant proving fluid clearance is complete
D. Excessive absorption of fetal lung liquid before birth

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Immaturity of epithelial sodium-mediated fluid absorption

The chapter links epithelial sodium transport, labor-related signals and maturation to lung liquid clearance.

## Authored MCQ

A late preterm infant feeds poorly and is discharged looking only mildly jaundiced. Which concern should shape follow-up?

A. Increased risk of important hyperbilirubinemia after discharge
B. Bilirubin risk ending when initial respiratory distress resolves
C. No need to assess intake once bilirubin is measured
D. Protection from jaundice because birth weight is normal

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increased risk of important hyperbilirubinemia after discharge

Late preterm bilirubin handling and feeding vulnerabilities increase jaundice and readmission risk.

PDF p. 723
</details>

## Authored MCQ

A 36-week infant has normal vital signs but repeated unsuccessful oral feeds. Which decision best fits the source discharge criteria?

A. Omit jaundice planning because feeding is the only concern
B. Use birth weight as the sole discharge criterion
C. Discharge because vital signs alone establish readiness
D. Continue assessment until successful feeding is documented

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Continue assessment until successful feeding is documented

The pathway requires successful feeding with coordinated sucking, swallowing and breathing, alongside other readiness criteria.
