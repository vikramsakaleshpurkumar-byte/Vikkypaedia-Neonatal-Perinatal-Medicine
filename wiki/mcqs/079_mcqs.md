## Authored MCQ

A newborn develops unexplained cyanosis or shock and a ductal-dependent lesion is strongly suspected while echocardiography is arranged. Which stabilization principle is described?

A. Promptly maintain ductal patency with prostaglandin E1 under monitoring
B. Assume oxygen alone corrects every obstructive lesion
C. Attempt pharmacologic ductal closure immediately
D. Wait for ductal closure before treating

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Promptly maintain ductal patency with prostaglandin E1 under monitoring

The chapter recommends prompt PGE1 while the diagnosis is confirmed because ductal flow may be lifesaving.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has inadequate mixing because an atrial communication is restrictive. Which catheter intervention directly addresses that limitation?

A. Ductal device closure
B. Balloon pulmonary valvuloplasty
C. Pulmonary artery banding
D. Balloon atrial septostomy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Balloon atrial septostomy

Septostomy enlarges the atrial communication to improve mixing or atrial egress as required by the anatomy.

## Authored MCQ

A neonate with selected single-ventricle physiology has excessive pulmonary flow before later repair or palliation. What is the aim of pulmonary artery banding?

A. Maximize an already excessive pulmonary shunt
B. Enlarge the atrial septum directly
C. Replace every definitive cardiac repair
D. Restrict pulmonary flow to improve circulatory balance

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Restrict pulmonary flow to improve circulatory balance

Banding is used selectively to limit pulmonary overcirculation, including some single-ventricle pathways.

## Authored MCQ

A neonate has systemic and pulmonary circulations connected by a ductus. Why should oxygen delivery and blood gases be managed with close attention to circulatory balance?

A. Every congenital lesion requires identical ventilation targets
B. Pulmonary and systemic resistances cannot affect flow distribution
C. Changing pulmonary resistance can redistribute flow away from systemic perfusion
D. A higher saturation always proves adequate systemic output

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Changing pulmonary resistance can redistribute flow away from systemic perfusion

Single-ventricle output must supply both beds; lowering pulmonary resistance may increase pulmonary flow at the expense of systemic delivery.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A team selects a drug with afterload reduction, increased contractility and improved relaxation. Which profile matches milrinone in the source?

A. Inotropic, lusitropic and afterload-reducing effects
B. Pure vasoconstriction without myocardial effects
C. Mechanical relief of an aortic obstruction
D. Only slowing atrial conduction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Inotropic, lusitropic and afterload-reducing effects

The combined effects can improve filling, stroke volume and output while reducing afterload.
