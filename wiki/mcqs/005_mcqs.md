## Authored MCQ

Which grouping matches the framework described for evaluating care quality?

A. Only expenditure and staffing
B. Only final mortality rates
C. Structure, process and outcome
D. Diagnosis, prognosis and genotype

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Structure, process and outcome

Structure concerns the care setting, process concerns care activities, and outcomes are their results.

## Authored MCQ

A project increases breast-milk feeding, but clinicians worry about growth velocity. Which approach is appropriate?

A. Wait until the project ends before looking for harm
B. Ignore growth because the primary target improved
C. Count milk provision as proof of every benefit
D. Track growth as a balancing measure alongside the target

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Track growth as a balancing measure alongside the target

Balancing measures detect related outcomes that may worsen while the target improves; the chapter gives this feeding-growth example.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A patient has a justified reason to depart from a shared care pathway. What approach matches the chapter?

A. Adapt care, document the deviation and learn from it
B. Keep deviations secret to protect the pathway
C. Discard standardization after one exception
D. Prohibit every deviation regardless of patient need

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Adapt care, document the deviation and learn from it

Shared baselines reduce unwarranted variation while allowing justified individualization and subsequent refinement.

## Authored MCQ

Two NICUs report different morbidity rates using different case definitions. What should precede a performance judgment?

A. Reconcile definitions and assess comparability
B. Rank the units solely by the reported percentages
C. Assume all databases define disease identically
D. Treat changing definitions as evidence of changing biology

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reconcile definitions and assess comparability

Clear, stable definitions across time and location are essential for interpreting differences and comparing NICUs.
