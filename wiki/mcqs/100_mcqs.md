## Authored MCQ

A premature retina initially has arrested vessel growth, then later develops pathologic neovascularization. Which mechanism fits the simplified model?

A. Both phases are caused by permanently absent VEGF
B. Early relative hyperoxia suppresses VEGF; later avascular retinal hypoxia raises VEGF
C. Late disease is unrelated to retinal metabolic demand
D. Normal vascularization is complete before extreme preterm birth

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Early relative hyperoxia suppresses VEGF; later avascular retinal hypoxia raises VEGF

Early growth suppression leaves avascular retina; later hypoxia stimulates excessive VEGF and abnormal vessels.

D. Use birth weight alone as a permanent substitute for retinal examination

ROP evolves with retinal maturation; repeated examinations detect progression while treatment can preserve vision.

## Authored MCQ

A retinal team plans laser for treatment-requiring disease. What is its main physiologic target?

A. Avascular peripheral retina overproducing angiogenic signals
B. The normal crystalline lens as the source of VEGF
C. All central vision as the intended therapeutic endpoint
D. The lacrimal sac to lower intraocular pressure

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Avascular peripheral retina overproducing angiogenic signals

Laser reduces the angiogenic drive from avascular retina to prevent progression and detachment, at the cost of peripheral retinal tissue.
