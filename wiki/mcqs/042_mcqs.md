## Authored MCQ

A stable very preterm infant receives small enteral volumes while parenteral nutrition supplies most nutrients. What is the principal enteral purpose?

A. Replace the need to monitor feeding tolerance
B. Supply every nutrient requirement immediately
C. Prevent all NEC regardless of other factors
D. Stimulate intestinal structural and functional maturation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Stimulate intestinal structural and functional maturation

Small volumes provide intestinal stimulation; the chapter also notes uncertainty in trial outcomes and optimal duration.

## Authored MCQ

An otherwise stable preterm infant has a gastric residual without other concerning findings. What conclusion is supported?

A. Routine fasting improves outcomes in every such infant
B. Residual volume alone does not justify stopping feeds
C. A residual proves a mechanical obstruction
D. Every residual establishes NEC

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Residual volume alone does not justify stopping feeds

The chapter finds no evidence supporting discontinuation solely for residuals in the absence of other symptoms.

## Authored MCQ

A preterm infant cannot receive sufficient maternal milk. Which alternative does the chapter recommend?

A. Unfortified donor milk assumed nutritionally complete
B. Pasteurized donor human milk with attention to nutrient adequacy
C. Unscreened unpasteurized milk from any donor
D. Water-based feeds until maternal supply increases

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pasteurized donor human milk with attention to nutrient adequacy

Pasteurized donor milk is recommended; fortification and growth monitoring address compositional differences.

## Authored MCQ

A very preterm infant receives human milk but grows poorly. Which explanation supports fortification?

A. All donor and maternal milk has an identical nutrient composition
B. Human milk contains no usable protein
C. Fortification is needed only to increase feed volume
D. Milk alone may not supply enough protein and minerals for preterm needs

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Milk alone may not supply enough protein and minerals for preterm needs

Preterm requirements can exceed milk supplies of protein, calcium, phosphorus and other nutrients; fortification improves growth.

## Authored MCQ

A small diet trial has a favorable secondary NEC signal but no difference in its primary outcome. Which interpretation is most defensible?

A. All fortification should cease because uncertainty remains
B. The primary outcome can be ignored after seeing any favorable result
C. Further direct comparisons are needed before declaring one fortifier superior
D. A secondary finding proves superiority for every outcome

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Further direct comparisons are needed before declaring one fortifier superior

The chapter distinguishes the trial’s primary PN-duration outcome from secondary findings and calls for well-designed head-to-head trials.
