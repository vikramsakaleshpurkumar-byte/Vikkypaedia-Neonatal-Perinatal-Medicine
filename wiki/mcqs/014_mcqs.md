## Authored MCQ

A newborn is below the tenth birth-weight percentile but has maintained an appropriate individual growth trajectory. Which distinction matters?

A. Every SGA infant has pathologic growth restriction
B. FGR requires birth weight below the tenth percentile in every case
C. SGA describes size and does not by itself prove FGR
D. Birth size alone identifies the cause of growth restriction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

SGA describes size and does not by itself prove FGR

SGA is a size category; constitutionally small infants can be SGA, and FGR can occur above the tenth percentile.

## Authored MCQ

Which mechanism most commonly underlies pathologic restriction of fetal growth in the source?

A. Congenital infection
B. Hypoxia and nutrient deprivation from placental insufficiency
C. An intrinsic inherited metabolic disorder
D. A fetal chromosomal disorder

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hypoxia and nutrient deprivation from placental insufficiency

Placental insufficiency limits fetal oxygen and nutrient delivery and is the commonest stated cause.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Serial imaging shows a small abdominal circumference with relatively preserved head circumference. Which pattern is most consistent?

A. A pattern excluding maternal environmental influences
B. A pattern proving normal placental function
C. Uniform enlargement of all fetal dimensions
D. Asymmetric head-sparing restriction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Asymmetric head-sparing restriction

Redistribution conserves delivery to brain and heart, producing relatively preserved head size and reduced weight or abdominal size.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has suspected pathologic prenatal growth deceleration. Which evaluation best addresses etiology?

A. Birth weight alone without gestational age
B. Discarding the placenta before reviewing the history
C. A normal head circumference as proof no restriction occurred
D. Pregnancy trajectory, anthropometry, examination and placental findings

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pregnancy trajectory, anthropometry, examination and placental findings

Prenatal pattern, maternal and family factors, physical findings and placental pathology can reveal the cause.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A growth-restricted newborn has low glucose after birth. Which substrate problem contributes?

A. Complete protection from hypoglycemia if born at term
B. Limited glycogen and fat reserves with impaired gluconeogenesis
C. Guaranteed compensatory ketone production
D. Uniformly abundant glycogen and fat reserves

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Limited glycogen and fat reserves with impaired gluconeogenesis

Reduced stores, impaired gluconeogenesis and sometimes limited ketone production increase vulnerability.

</details>
