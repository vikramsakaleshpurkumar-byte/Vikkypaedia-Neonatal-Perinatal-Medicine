## Authored MCQ

A premature infant’s lungs have reduced compliance and diffuse atelectasis. Which initiating mechanism best explains the classic syndrome?

A. Primary upper-airway closure as the defining lesion
B. Excess surfactant making all alveoli rigid
C. Complete absence of pulmonary blood flow in every case
D. Insufficient surfactant synthesis or secretion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Insufficient surfactant synthesis or secretion

Surfactant deficiency promotes collapse, hypoventilation and ventilation-perfusion imbalance; injury and protein leak can further inactivate surfactant.

## Authored MCQ

Preterm delivery appears likely and the obstetric team evaluates fetal lung maturation treatment. Which drug class has the strongest support in

A. Routine antenatal antibiotics as a direct surfactant inducer
B. Postnatal diuretics administered to the mother
C. Maternal bronchodilator therapy as an equivalent substitute
D. Antenatal corticosteroids

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Antenatal corticosteroids

Antenatal corticosteroids induce developmentally regulated proteins and reduce incidence and severity of RDS in appropriate preterm-risk settings.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A very preterm infant develops early distress. Which radiographic combination most supports surfactant-deficient disease?

A. A unilateral overexpanded lobe with a focal mass
B. Only a large pleural air collection without parenchymal change
C. Isolated bowel loops within the thorax
D. Low volumes, diffuse ground-glass pattern and air bronchograms

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Low volumes, diffuse ground-glass pattern and air bronchograms

Diffuse atelectasis produces ground-glass opacity with aerated bronchi against poorly aerated alveoli.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant with established surfactant-deficient disease receives replacement therapy. Which expectation is supported?

A. Reduced RDS mortality and pneumothorax risk, without guaranteed prevention of BPD
B. Guaranteed elimination of all later chronic lung disease
C. A treatment that replaces all respiratory monitoring
D. No improvement in aeration is possible

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced RDS mortality and pneumothorax risk, without guaranteed prevention of BPD

Surfactant replacement improves important acute outcomes; BPD remains multifactorial and can follow limited early RDS.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A spontaneously breathing preterm infant remains on nasal CPAP while surfactant is delivered through a thin tracheal catheter. Which approach is described?

A. Nebulization without tracheal access
B. Intravenous surfactant infusion
C. INSURE with a period of endotracheal ventilation by definition
D. LISA or MIST

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

LISA or MIST

LISA/MIST aims to deliver surfactant while preserving spontaneous breathing and CPAP support, limiting invasive ventilation exposure.

</details>
