## Authored MCQ

A posterior spinal sac contains meninges while the cord remains normally positioned in the spinal canal. Which lesion fits?

A. Anencephaly
B. Lipomyelomeningocele defined by tethering fat
C. Myelomeningocele containing the neural placode
D. Meningocele

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Meningocele

In meningocele, meninges herniate but the spinal cord and roots remain within the canal.

## Authored MCQ

A skin-covered lumbosacral lesion is associated with a cord-tethering lipoma. Which principle applies?

A. Closed dysraphism can tether neural tissue despite intact skin
B. Skin coverage excludes all spinal abnormalities
C. Neurologic assessment is unnecessary until adulthood
D. Every closed lesion contains a normal untethered cord

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Closed dysraphism can tether neural tissue despite intact skin

Closed lesions include lipomyelomeningocele and other tethering disorders.

MRI offers detailed soft-tissue anatomy as ossification limits ultrasound access.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has an exposed open spinal defect. Which initial priority fits the source approach?

A. Leave the neural tissue exposed until elective review
B. Manage as a cosmetic skin lesion alone
C. Protect the lesion with sterile covering and coordinate prompt specialist management
D. Postpone infection prevention until signs of meningitis appear

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Protect the lesion with sterile covering and coordinate prompt specialist management

Exposed neural tissue and CSF create infection risk; sterile protection, indicated antimicrobial care and surgical coordination are central.
