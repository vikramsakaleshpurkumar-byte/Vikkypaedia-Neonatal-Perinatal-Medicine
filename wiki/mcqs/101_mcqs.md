## Authored MCQ

An abnormality arises when a structurally formed limb is constrained later in pregnancy. Which category best fits?

A. A necessary chromosomal deletion
B. Deformation of an essentially formed part
C. An early embryonic failure of differentiation by definition
D. A mandatory postnatal fracture

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Deformation of an essentially formed part

Early embryonic disturbances produce malformations; later fetal influences can alter the configuration of essentially normal structures.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn from a pregnancy with oligohydramnios has positional lower-limb abnormalities. What mechanism is plausible?

A. Complete protection from persistent hip disease
B. Increased uterine compression affecting fetal position and motion
C. A necessary neonatal bacterial infection
D. Proof that every affected bone failed to form

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increased uterine compression affecting fetal position and motion

Oligohydramnios and other constraint factors may produce contractures and angular or torsional changes; some resolve, others require care.

## Authored MCQ

After a difficult delivery, an infant holds an arm adducted and internally rotated with an extended elbow and pronated forearm. Which pattern is most consistent?

A. Isolated lower plexus palsy affecting only hand function
B. A normal symmetric neonatal posture
C. A proximal humeral fracture causing painful pseudoparalysis
D. Upper brachial plexus palsy

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Upper brachial plexus palsy

The described waiter’s-tip posture is characteristic of the common upper plexus injury pattern.

## Authored MCQ

A baby has irritability, tenderness and crepitation over the clavicle after delivery and moves the arm little. What mechanism best explains reduced movement?

A. Proof of permanent nerve-root avulsion
B. Normal lack of movement in every neonate
C. A complete exclusion of skeletal injury
D. Pain-related pseudoparalysis from fracture

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Pain-related pseudoparalysis from fracture

Clavicular fracture can cause painful reduced motion and mimic a neurologic palsy; examination and imaging distinguish causes.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has shoulder pain and swelling after delivery, but the initial radiograph is nondiagnostic. What anatomic limitation matters?

A. Every neonatal epiphysis is fully ossified
B. A normal initial radiograph excludes all separation
C. Ultrasound cannot assess any cartilaginous neonatal structure
D. The proximal humeral epiphysis is not yet ossified

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The proximal humeral epiphysis is not yet ossified

An unossified epiphysis can conceal separation on plain radiography; ultrasound or other imaging and later callus may help.

</details>
