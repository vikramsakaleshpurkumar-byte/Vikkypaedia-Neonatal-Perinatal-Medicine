## Authored MCQ

A very preterm infant is evaluated for suspected invasive fungal infection. Which sampling approach fits the chapter?

A. Blood, urine and CSF cultures as clinically appropriate
B. Blood culture alone to exclude every organ infection
C. An airway culture interpreted as definitive bloodstream infection
D. A skin swab alone to establish disseminated disease

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Blood, urine and CSF cultures as clinically appropriate

Sterile-site cultures remain central; urine and CSF may reveal infection not shown by blood culture.

## Authored MCQ

An infant with suspected disseminated fungal disease has normal CSF cell counts. Which conclusion is supported?

A. Brain tissue and CSF infection are always identical in distribution
B. CSF culture still matters because routine indices may be normal
C. Normal counts exclude meningitis in every case
D. Negative blood cultures make CSF evaluation unnecessary

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

CSF culture still matters because routine indices may be normal

CNS candidiasis can occur without candidemia and without abnormal routine CSF chemistries or counts.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Candida is isolated from a central-line-dependent infant’s blood. What additional management priority is supported?

A. Assume antifungals always penetrate catheter biofilms fully
B. Prompt removal of the implicated central catheter when feasible
C. Ignore source control if species identification is pending
D. Retain the line routinely until all therapy is completed

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Prompt removal of the implicated central catheter when feasible

Prompt removal is associated with improved clearance and outcomes; Candida biofilms can resist drug penetration.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Blood cultures remain positive during treatment. Which evaluation addresses a plausible persistent focus?

A. Assume persistence reflects harmless colonization
B. Look for infected thrombi and end-organ abscesses
C. Stop looking for organ involvement once one blood culture is positive
D. Use only skin inspection to exclude dissemination

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Look for infected thrombi and end-organ abscesses

Persistent candidemia increases concern for dissemination, including catheter-tip thrombi and abdominal or brain foci.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

Two primary maternal infections occur at different gestations. Which relationship does the source describe?

A. Transmission falls later and later infection is always more severe
B. Transmission rises later, while earlier fetal infection tends to be more severe
C. Timing has no effect on either transmission or severity
D. Every maternal infection causes identical fetal disease

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Transmission rises later, while earlier fetal infection tends to be more severe

The probability of transmission and severity move in different directions with gestation.

PDF p. 917
</details>
