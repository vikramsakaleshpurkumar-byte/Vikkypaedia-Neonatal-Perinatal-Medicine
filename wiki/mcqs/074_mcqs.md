## Authored MCQ

A screening study shows four chambers but has not assessed outflow tracts or pulmonary venous connections. What additional approach is appropriate?

A. Assess only chamber size on repeat imaging
B. Systematic segmental assessment including outflow tracts, arches and venous return
C. Replace Doppler assessment with fetal heart rate alone
D. Declare all congenital heart disease excluded

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Systematic segmental assessment including outflow tracts, arches and venous return

A fetal echocardiogram evaluates anatomy, connections, outflow tracts, arches and functional flow using two-dimensional and Doppler imaging.

## Authored MCQ

A program limits cardiac assessment to families with known congenital heart disease, teratogen exposure or increased nuchal translucency. Which limitation should be recognized?

A. Family history alone identifies every major lesion
B. These groups contain essentially all affected pregnancies
C. Absence of a teratogen exposure excludes structural disease
D. Many affected babies do not belong to these traditional high-risk groups

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Many affected babies do not belong to these traditional high-risk groups

The chapter estimates that only a minority of affected babies would be identified by restricting echocardiography to traditional high-risk groups.

## Authored MCQ

Parents ask whether a normal fetal echocardiogram guarantees absence of a small ventricular defect or bicuspid aortic valve. Which response is most accurate?

A. Small defects and minor valve abnormalities cannot be reliably excluded
B. Only rhythm disorders can escape detection
C. Every missed lesion must reflect technical negligence
D. A normal study excludes every cardiac lesion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Small defects and minor valve abnormalities cannot be reliably excluded

Small structures are less readily assessed; small VSDs, partial anomalous pulmonary venous return and minor valve abnormalities may not be excluded.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A fetus has a lesion expected to require urgent neonatal intervention. What is the most useful consequence of prenatal diagnosis?

A. Guarantee normal neonatal physiology
B. Use the same delivery resources for every cardiac lesion
C. Coordinate level of care, cardiology access, transport and likely intervention
D. Delay all planning until symptoms develop after birth

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Coordinate level of care, cardiology access, transport and likely intervention

Disease-specific findings permit risk-stratified delivery planning to prevent hemodynamic compromise.

## Authored MCQ

A distressed parent cannot absorb all the information at the first discussion. What approach matches the chapter?

A. Withhold all uncertainty until after birth
B. Interpret distress as evidence that counseling is futile
C. Assume one detailed statistical explanation is sufficient
D. Reassess understanding and provide repeated empathetic counseling

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reassess understanding and provide repeated empathetic counseling

Counseling includes findings, limitations, prognosis, management options and support, with understanding reassessed and sessions repeated as needed.
