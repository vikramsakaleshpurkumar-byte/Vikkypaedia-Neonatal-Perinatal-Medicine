A newborn appears temporarily more stable after resuscitation. Why can neuronal injury still progress?

A. An early normal appearance guarantees no later encephalopathy
B. Secondary injury is unrelated to metabolism
C. Secondary mitochondrial energy failure and delayed cell injury can follow the initial insult
D. All injury stops immediately when circulation returns

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Secondary mitochondrial energy failure and delayed cell injury can follow the initial insult

The source describes delayed energy failure after the initial insult, supporting early assessment and intervention.

A. Initiation within 6 hours of birth
B. Routine initiation after the first week
C. Only after developmental impairment is proven
D. Delay until MRI at term-equivalent age

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Initiation within 6 hours of birth

The cited trials began hypothermia within 6 hours and demonstrated reduced death or severe disability in eligible mature infants.

A. All low scores imply an identical brain injury pattern

Early Apgar scores have limited long-term predictive sensitivity; hypothermia also changes interpretation of older prognostic data.
