## Authored MCQ

Imaging shows an aorta arising from the right ventricle and a pulmonary artery arising from the left ventricle. Why can a restrictive atrial communication cause profound hypoxemia?

A. Parallel circulations require mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
B. The circulation is normally in series with complete mixing
C. A large atrial communication necessarily blocks systemic output
D. Pulmonary venous blood already enters the aorta directly

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Parallel circulations require mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood

The two circuits run in parallel; atrial mixing is particularly effective and may be essential to adequate systemic oxygenation.

All pulmonary veins join channels returning to systemic veins rather than entering the left atrium. Which anatomic diagnosis fits?

A. Dextro-transposition of the great arteries
B. Total anomalous pulmonary venous connection
C. Isolated valvar pulmonary stenosis
D. Isolated ventricular septal defect

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Total anomalous pulmonary venous connection

All pulmonary venous blood returns anomalously to the systemic venous circulation; variants depend on the level of drainage.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has severe left ventricular and aortic hypoplasia. As the ductus closes, perfusion falls and metabolic acidosis develops. What mechanism best explains this?

A. A mandatory increase in systemic oxygen delivery as saturation rises
B. Loss of right-ventricular supply to the systemic circulation through the ductus
C. Loss of the only normal route of pulmonary venous return
D. Sudden restoration of a normal-sized left ventricle

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Loss of right-ventricular supply to the systemic circulation through the ductus

The right ventricle supplies systemic flow through the ductus, including retrograde arch and coronary flow; closure can cause profound hypoperfusion.

## Authored MCQ

An infant develops poor feeding, acidosis and weak femoral pulses after initially appearing well. Which lesion should be urgently considered?

A. Small restrictive muscular ventricular septal defect
B. Critical aortic coarctation
C. Small isolated atrial septal defect
D. Mild isolated pulmonary valve stenosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Critical aortic coarctation

Critical arch obstruction can present with systemic hypoperfusion as ductal support disappears; pulse differences are important clues.

## Authored MCQ

A baby with a large ventricular communication becomes increasingly tachypneic during the weeks after birth. Which hemodynamic change explains the timing?

A. The ventricular defect necessarily closing as symptoms worsen
B. Increasing pulmonary resistance eliminating the shunt
C. Increasing left-to-right shunt as pulmonary resistance decreases
D. An atrial communication becoming the only systemic outlet

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Increasing left-to-right shunt as pulmonary resistance decreases

Falling pulmonary resistance promotes a larger left-to-right shunt and pulmonary overcirculation.
