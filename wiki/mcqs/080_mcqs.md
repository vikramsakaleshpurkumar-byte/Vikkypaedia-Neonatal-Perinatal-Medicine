## Authored MCQ

A stable premature infant has lower vitamin K-dependent factor levels than adult reference values. Which interpretation is best?

A. Every lower factor value establishes a bleeding disorder
B. Adult ranges are adequate for every gestation
C. Natural anticoagulants are always at adult levels at birth
D. Developmental hemostasis differs from adults and requires neonatal interpretation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Developmental hemostasis differs from adults and requires neonatal interpretation

Several procoagulants and anticoagulants are lower in neonates; counterbalancing differences and age-specific ranges matter.

## Authored MCQ

A well term infant has severe thrombocytopenia and purpura soon after birth. The mother has antibodies against a platelet antigen inherited from the father. What mechanism fits?

A. An isolated neonatal lack of factor VIII
B. Maternal IgG targeting fetal platelets bearing a paternal antigen
C. A necessary maternal deficiency of every clotting factor
D. Physiologic low platelet count in every term infant

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Maternal IgG targeting fetal platelets bearing a paternal antigen

Transplacental maternal alloantibodies bind incompatible fetal platelet antigens and promote platelet clearance.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A neonate has recurrent umbilical bleeding and impaired wound healing despite normal PT and aPTT. Which deficiency remains important to investigate?

A. Factor VII deficiency
B. Severe factor X deficiency
C. Factor XIII deficiency
D. Severe prothrombin deficiency

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Factor XIII deficiency

Factor XIII deficiency is an exception in which routine PT and aPTT can remain normal; specific quantitative assays are needed.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

An infant without birth prophylaxis develops bleeding and suspected vitamin K deficiency. Which screening pattern is described as the earliest laboratory evidence?

A. Isolated PT prolongation before aPTT prolongation
B. A shortened PT
C. Isolated thrombocytopenia with no factor effects
D. Normal PT and aPTT in all severe cases

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Isolated PT prolongation before aPTT prolongation

PT prolongation is the earliest screening abnormality, with aPTT prolongation as deficiency progresses.

## Authored MCQ

A septic neonate develops oozing, falling platelets and organ dysfunction. Which mechanism unifies the findings?

A. An isolated deficiency of factor VIII
B. A purely local wound problem without systemic activation
C. Only impaired platelet production without clot formation
D. Systemic coagulation activation with factor consumption and microvascular thrombosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Systemic coagulation activation with factor consumption and microvascular thrombosis

DIC combines coagulation and fibrinolytic activation, consumption of platelets and factors, and fibrin deposition that may impair organs.

</details>
