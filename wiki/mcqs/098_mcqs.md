## Authored MCQ

A very premature infant has fragile skin. Which pair of functions explains why skin injury can affect overall physiology?

A. Barrier to water loss and infection control
B. Thermoregulation alone with no water-barrier role
C. Pigmentation alone with no chemical-barrier role
D. Tactile sensing alone with no host-defense role

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Barrier to water loss and infection control

Skin supports water balance, thermoregulation and host defense; injury can therefore have systemic consequences.

## Authored MCQ

A well term infant develops transient papules and pustules on an erythematous base after the first day, with no systemic illness. A smear shows many eosinophils and no bacteria. Which diagnosis fits?

A. Transient neonatal pustular melanosis with predominantly residual pigmented macules
B. Inherited epidermolysis bullosa
C. Disseminated bacterial infection established by eosinophils alone
D. Erythema toxicum

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Erythema toxicum

The source describes an evanescent, sterile, eosinophil-rich benign eruption, usually arising after birth.

## Authored MCQ

A well newborn has superficial pustules with little surrounding redness, collarettes of scale and brown macules already present at birth. Which diagnosis best fits?

A. A primary inherited skin-fragility disorder
B. Classic erythema toxicum arising only after the first day
C. Transient neonatal pustular melanosis
D. A congenital melanocytic nevus as the explanation for every pustule

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Transient neonatal pustular melanosis

The eruption progresses from superficial pustules to collarettes and residual pigmented macules, and stages may coexist at birth.

The source describes genetic diagnosis, with immunofluorescence mapping or electron microscopy reserved for selected situations.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has annular periorbital lesions and maternal anti-Ro/SSA antibodies. Which mechanism explains the neonatal syndrome?

A. A primary zinc-transporter mutation in every case
B. An obligatory neonatal infection acquired during skin examination
C. Transplacental maternal autoantibodies
D. Direct inheritance of maternal skin lesions as tissue

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Transplacental maternal autoantibodies

Maternal anti-Ro/SSA or anti-La/SSB antibodies can produce transient cutaneous disease and congenital heart block; mothers may be asymptomatic.

PDF p. 2014
</details>
