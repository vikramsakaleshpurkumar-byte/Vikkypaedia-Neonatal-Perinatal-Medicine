## Authored MCQ

A newborn has a low serum sodium concentration. Why cannot this value alone define extracellular volume?

A. Water balance has no influence on serum sodium
B. Concentration reflects sodium relative to water, while total sodium content influences extracellular volume
C. Every low concentration proves excess total sodium
D. Concentration directly measures all body sodium stores

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Concentration reflects sodium relative to water, while total sodium content influences extracellular volume

The concentration is a salt-water relationship; total sodium content is a major determinant of extracellular volume.

## Authored MCQ

A very small premature infant loses more water than a term infant in a similar thermal setting. Which mechanism is important?

A. Only urinary sodium loss with no skin component
B. Greater transepidermal evaporative loss through immature skin
C. Complete absence of respiratory water loss in term infants
D. An inability of environmental humidity to affect evaporation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Greater transepidermal evaporative loss through immature skin

Immature skin contributes substantially to evaporative loss; environment and treatments also influence requirements.

A premature infant develops low sodium. Which framework best directs evaluation?

A. Only excess sodium intake
B. Only a primary respiratory disorder
C. A single universal mechanism in every preterm infant
D. Water retention, excessive sodium loss or inadequate sodium intake

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Water retention, excessive sodium loss or inadequate sodium intake

The chapter classifies hyponatremia through water handling, sodium losses and intake; clinical volume assessment separates them.

A blood gas has low bicarbonate. Which approach avoids mislabeling compensation as a primary metabolic disorder?

A. Identify the primary abnormality, secondary compensation and whether compensation is appropriate
B. Ignore pH once bicarbonate is known
C. Label every low bicarbonate as isolated primary metabolic acidosis
D. Assume a mixed disorder is impossible in neonates

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Identify the primary abnormality, secondary compensation and whether compensation is appropriate

pH, bicarbonate and carbon dioxide together establish the primary process and adequacy of compensation; mixed disorders occur.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has repeated gastric losses and contraction alkalosis. Why may alkalosis persist after acid loss has initiated it?

A. Volume and chloride depletion favor bicarbonate retention and impaired alkali excretion
B. The kidney cannot respond to extracellular volume
C. A low potassium concentration necessarily corrects alkalosis
D. Volume depletion always maximizes bicarbonate excretion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Volume and chloride depletion favor bicarbonate retention and impaired alkali excretion

Reduced filtration and enhanced sodium-bicarbonate reabsorption, together with hormonal effects, help maintain alkalosis.
