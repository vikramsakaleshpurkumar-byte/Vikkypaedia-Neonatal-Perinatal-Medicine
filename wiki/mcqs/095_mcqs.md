## Authored MCQ

Two infants have a similar total unconjugated bilirubin concentration but different binding capacity. Why may their risk differ?

A. Total bilirubin always determines tissue exposure independently of binding
B. Unconjugated bilirubin is freely water soluble at physiologic pH
C. Albumin converts all bilirubin into a water-soluble conjugate
D. Albumin binding influences the unbound fraction available to enter tissues

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Albumin binding influences the unbound fraction available to enter tissues

Unconjugated bilirubin is poorly water soluble and circulates bound to albumin; binding capacity influences biologic availability.

## Authored MCQ

A severely jaundiced newborn develops poor feeding, lethargy, hypotonia and a high-pitched cry, followed by arching. Which complication is concerning?

A. Acute bilirubin encephalopathy
B. A benign feeding preference
C. Isolated biliary obstruction with no neurologic risk assessment
D. Uncomplicated physiologic jaundice without neurologic involvement

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Acute bilirubin encephalopathy

This constellation is described in acute bilirubin encephalopathy and calls for urgent management of severe hyperbilirubinemia.

## Authored MCQ

A neonate receives phototherapy for unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia. Which process explains its effect?

A. Photoisomerization including formation of more readily eliminated bilirubin isomers
B. Albumin synthesis by the light source
C. Mechanical removal of an obstructed bile duct
D. Direct replacement of all circulating red cells

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Photoisomerization including formation of more readily eliminated bilirubin isomers

Light changes bilirubin configuration and structure, including formation of lumirubin, facilitating elimination.

## Authored MCQ

A persistently jaundiced infant has a rise in conjugated bilirubin. Which interpretation is appropriate?

A. A normal variant requiring no investigation
B. A finding explained solely by ordinary unconjugated physiologic jaundice
C. Proof of one specific obstructive diagnosis without further testing
D. A pathologic bile secretion or flow problem requiring evaluation

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A pathologic bile secretion or flow problem requiring evaluation

The chapter calls conjugated hyperbilirubinemia always pathologic; its differential includes obstructive, hepatocellular, infectious and metabolic disease.
