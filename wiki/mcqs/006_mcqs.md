## Authored MCQ

A trainee needs to practise a rare emergency skill before performing it on an infant. Which educational strategy best fits the chapter?

A. Replacing practice with attendance records alone
B. Assuming one lecture establishes competence
C. Waiting for the first critically ill infant
D. Repeated practice in a safe simulated setting

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Repeated practice in a safe simulated setting

Simulation permits repeated practice and remediation before patient exposure, consistent with avoiding preventable harm.

## Authored MCQ

Two learners attend the same number of sessions but perform differently. What assessment principle follows?

A. Declare equal competence from equal hours
B. Use seniority as the only performance measure
C. Judge demonstrated performance rather than attendance alone
D. Ignore individual learning needs

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Judge demonstrated performance rather than attendance alone

Learners have different experience, strengths and learning rates; time spent does not by itself show successful performance.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

After a scenario, team members discuss events with each other and the facilitator. What is this activity?

A. Pre-scenario briefing
B. One-way device feedback alone
C. Attendance certification
D. Debriefing

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Debriefing

Debriefing discusses completed events through interaction; briefing concerns planned events, and feedback can flow one way.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A scenario in the actual NICU reveals that essential equipment cannot be found quickly. What benefit is illustrated?

A. Evidence that workplace context does not matter
B. Proof that every learner lacks clinical knowledge
C. Identification of a latent safety threat
D. A guarantee that future adverse events are impossible

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Identification of a latent safety threat

In situ simulation can uncover equipment, personnel and communication problems in the actual clinical environment.
