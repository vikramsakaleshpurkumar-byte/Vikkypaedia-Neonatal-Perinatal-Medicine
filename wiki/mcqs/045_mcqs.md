## Authored MCQ

Which drug profile most favors passive placental transfer?

A. A molecule unable to dissolve in lipids but completely protein-bound
B. Large, fully ionized and tightly protein-bound
C. Small, lipid-soluble, largely unionized and weakly protein-bound
D. High molecular weight alone regardless of all other properties

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Small, lipid-soluble, largely unionized and weakly protein-bound

These properties favor movement across placental membranes by passive diffusion.

## Authored MCQ

Two patients receive the same mg/kg of a water-soluble drug. Why may the newborn have a lower initial plasma peak?

A. Renal maturity identical to the adult
B. Complete absence of tissue distribution
C. A larger volume of distribution per kilogram
D. A smaller extracellular water fraction

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

A larger volume of distribution per kilogram

Newborns have relatively more total and extracellular body water, increasing distribution of hydrophilic drugs.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has reduced albumin binding of a drug. At an unchanged total concentration, what may increase?

A. Assurance that an adult target concentration is safe
B. The unbound pharmacologically active fraction
C. The bound fraction by definition
D. Albumin binding affinity in every newborn

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

The unbound pharmacologically active fraction

Reduced binding increases the fraction available to exert pharmacologic effects; total adult ranges need cautious interpretation.

## Authored MCQ

A premature infant clears caffeine much more slowly than an adult. Which developmental feature contributes?

A. Absence of any renal contribution to neonatal clearance
B. Fully mature CYP1A2 at birth
C. Delayed CYP1A2 activity
D. An obligatory zero-order pathway for all neonatal drugs

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Delayed CYP1A2 activity

The primary caffeine demethylation pathway involves CYP1A2, which develops slowly after birth.

</details>

## Authored MCQ

A neonate receiving gentamicin starts indomethacin. Which pharmacokinetic change warrants attention?

A. Gentamicin elimination becomes independent of the kidney
B. Indomethacin invariably doubles renal filtration
C. Reduced renal perfusion can decrease gentamicin clearance
D. Renal function changes cannot affect antimicrobial exposure

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduced renal perfusion can decrease gentamicin clearance

Prostaglandin inhibition can increase renal vascular resistance and reduce clearance of filtration-dependent drugs.

</details>
