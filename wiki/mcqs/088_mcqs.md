## Authored MCQ

A five-week-old has progressive nonbilious projectile vomiting, dehydration and hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis. Which diagnosis best fits?

A. Congenital chloride diarrhea with high stool chloride
B. Hirschsprung disease presenting with absent stool passage
C. Midgut volvulus with bilious emesis
D. Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis

Progressive nonbilious projectile emesis in early infancy with contraction alkalosis is the classic pattern.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has intrinsic duodenal obstruction. Which developmental process is implicated in the source?

A. Failure of recanalization after epithelial proliferation
B. Postnatal hypertrophy of the pyloric muscle alone
C. Failure of all neural crest migration into the colon
D. A fetal mesenteric vascular interruption

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Failure of recanalization after epithelial proliferation

The embryonic duodenum transiently becomes solid; failure of recanalization is linked to intrinsic atresia or stenosis.

## Authored MCQ

An infant has abnormal intestinal rotation with a narrow mesenteric attachment. What dangerous event can result?

A. Isolated gastric acid excess without vascular effects
B. Guaranteed protection against intestinal obstruction
C. Twisting of the mesentery compromising bowel blood supply
D. Spontaneous enlargement of every mesenteric vessel

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Twisting of the mesentery compromising bowel blood supply

Improper fixation permits twisting around the mesenteric vascular axis and can compromise perfusion.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn has abdominal distension, vomiting and failure to pass meconium. Testing identifies a distal bowel segment without enteric ganglion cells. What is the underlying process?

A. A primary excessive colonic water absorption without innervation abnormality
B. Hypertrophy of the gastric pyloric muscle
C. Arrested development of the myenteric nervous system
D. A defect limited to pancreatic enzyme release

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Arrested development of the myenteric nervous system

Congenital intestinal aganglionosis produces a functionally obstructed distal segment.

## Authored MCQ

A previously feeding newborn suddenly develops green emesis. Which diagnosis must be urgently excluded?

A. Typical isolated hypertrophic pyloric stenosis
B. Malrotation with midgut volvulus
C. Uncomplicated physiologic regurgitation
D. Benign swallowed air alone

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Malrotation with midgut volvulus

The chapter emphasizes urgent exclusion of malrotation and volvulus because delay can allow ischemic bowel injury.
