## Authored MCQ

Which antibody class explains passive fetal-neonatal disease after maternal antibody transfer?

A. IgE
B. IgA
C. IgM
D. IgG

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

IgG

Maternal IgG crosses the placenta, unlike IgM and IgA in the source’s description.

## Authored MCQ

A mother lacks a platelet antigen that her fetus inherited from the father. She produces antibodies that destroy fetal platelets. Which mechanism fits?

A. A nonimmune lack of megakaryocytes in every affected fetus
B. Fetal IgM crossing into the mother and returning unchanged
C. Maternal antibodies directed only against her own platelets in every case
D. Maternal IgG against a fetal platelet alloantigen

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Maternal IgG against a fetal platelet alloantigen

Maternal sensitization to an antigen-positive fetus produces IgG that crosses back and destroys fetal platelets.

</details>

D. A paternal platelet antigen cannot be relevant

The source reports that many FNAIT cases occur during the first pregnancy of at-risk couples.
