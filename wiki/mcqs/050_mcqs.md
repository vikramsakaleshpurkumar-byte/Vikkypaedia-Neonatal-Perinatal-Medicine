## Authored MCQ

A sepsis evaluation yields only a tiny blood specimen. Which limitation should affect interpretation of a negative result?

A. Any volume guarantees identical sensitivity
B. Low specimen volume can miss low-level bacteremia
C. Small samples identify the pathogen more rapidly in every case
D. A negative low-volume sample excludes meningitis

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Low specimen volume can miss low-level bacteremia

The chapter emphasizes sufficient volume to improve detection when organism concentrations are low.

## Authored MCQ

A neonate remains clinically concerning for meningitis despite a negative blood culture. What principle applies?

A. Lumbar puncture must proceed immediately despite profound instability
B. Normal blood leukocytes prove the CSF is sterile
C. Negative blood culture excludes all CNS infection
D. Blood results alone should not determine whether CSF evaluation is needed

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Blood results alone should not determine whether CSF evaluation is needed

Blood and CSF cultures correlate imperfectly; lumbar puncture is considered according to suspicion and stability.
