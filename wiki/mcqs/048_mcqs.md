## Authored MCQ

An exposed newborn has tremulousness, difficulty settling, sweating and loose stools. Which grouping fits the typical pattern?

A. CNS irritability, autonomic overactivity and gastrointestinal dysfunction
B. Uniform coma without feeding disturbance
C. Only renal failure with no behavioral changes
D. Isolated flaccid paralysis without autonomic findings

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

CNS irritability, autonomic overactivity and gastrointestinal dysfunction

Opioid receptor distribution in the nervous system and gut helps explain the clinical domains.

## Authored MCQ

An opioid-exposed infant develops poor feeding and temperature instability. What is the best diagnostic principle?

A. Assume a positive test proves the cause of every sign
B. Exclude hypoglycemia solely from the exposure history
C. Attribute all abnormalities to withdrawal immediately
D. Consider infection and metabolic or neurologic causes as well

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Consider infection and metabolic or neurologic causes as well

Withdrawal can mimic infection, glucose or calcium disturbances and neurologic injury; concurrent causes need consideration.

## Authored MCQ

A newborn’s urine screen is negative several days after delivery despite a convincing exposure history. Which explanation is supported?

A. Urine reliably records every exposure throughout gestation
B. Rapid elimination can make urine testing miss earlier exposure
C. The infant cannot have withdrawal without a positive urine test
D. A negative urine test excludes all prenatal exposure

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Rapid elimination can make urine testing miss earlier exposure

Many drugs clear rapidly; specimen type and timing determine the detection window.

## Authored MCQ

A stable exposed infant is unsettled by bright light and noise but feeds with support. Which care plan fits?

A. Reduce stimuli, swaddle, comfort and support frequent appropriate feeds
B. Withhold feeding until all irritability stops
C. Separate the infant routinely from responsive caregivers
D. Increase stimulation to exhaust the infant

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Reduce stimuli, swaddle, comfort and support frequent appropriate feeds

Supportive care targets comfort, nutrition, sleep and growth while watching for dehydration and weight loss.
