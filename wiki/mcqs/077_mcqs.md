## Authored MCQ

An infant has normally lateralized abdominal organs. Which conclusion about cardiac anatomy is justified?

A. The position of the stomach determines every arterial connection
B. Normal visceral arrangement does not guarantee normal cardiac connections
C. Normal abdominal situs excludes major heart disease
D. Dextrocardia and situs inversus are interchangeable terms

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Normal visceral arrangement does not guarantee normal cardiac connections

Situs describes lateralization; concordance or discordance of cardiac connections requires separate evaluation.

## Authored MCQ

A neonate has high-output cardiac failure, a cranial bruit, macrocephaly and systemic diastolic runoff. Which cause best fits?

A. Isolated sinus bradycardia
B. Isolated small ventricular septal defect
C. Vein of Galen aneurysmal malformation
D. Small slowly accumulating pericardial effusion

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Vein of Galen aneurysmal malformation

A large cerebral arteriovenous shunt can divert systemic flow and produce high-output failure, a bruit and hydrocephalus.

## Authored MCQ

A baby with a central venous catheter abruptly develops tachycardia and poor perfusion. Echocardiography shows pericardial fluid and chamber collapse. Which mechanism is most concerning?

A. Normal fetal fluid physiology requiring no reassessment
B. A small volume excluding hemodynamic compromise
C. An isolated increase in effective preload
D. Rapid accumulation causing cardiac tamponade

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Rapid accumulation causing cardiac tamponade

Rapid accumulation can raise intrapericardial pressure and impair filling even when fluid volume is small; central lines are a common iatrogenic cause.

</details>

An infant has stridor, recurrent respiratory symptoms and feeding difficulty. Imaging is requested to assess external airway and esophageal compression. Which category is being investigated?

A. Vascular ring or sling
B. Transient sinus arrhythmia
C. Isolated atrial septal defect
D. Isolated small patent foramen ovale

<details><summary>Answer and rationale</summary>

Vascular ring or sling

Vascular rings may compress the airway and esophagus, producing combined respiratory and feeding symptoms.
